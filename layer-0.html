Main Layer (0)

The Metropolis  ·  Post-Intervention Enforcement

Main Layer (0) is where every citizen begins. It is the largest ring by population, the default starting point for all voluntary entrants, and the civic center around which the rest of the civilization is organized. The system here lets harm occur and ensures that it produces real and permanent consequence, which is what makes the freedom genuine.

Overview & Purpose

Citizens live here with full economic support, full personal autonomy, and full access to the technologies that make VMSS distinct: neural diving, biological augmentation, backup vessel continuity, and the STI system. Harmful acts can complete because the system holds that moral character can only be demonstrated where choices carry real weight. The safety in +1 is earned, and Main Layer is where the earning happens. Together, Main Layer and Sanctuary form a symbiotic upper pair: residents who maintain sustained compliance ascend to +1, while Sanctuary residents whose STI drops below the maintenance threshold phase back to Main. The border between the two is fluid and non-stigmatized; phased-back residents face no penalty and rebuild from Main Layer on the same terms as everyone else.

Key Characteristics

  • Economy & Abundance: The same $10,000/month UBI as Sanctuary, in the same currency, on the same post-scarcity baseline. A Main Layer resident on UBI alone lives comfortably, with housing, food, healthcare, and discretionary spending covered without strain. A resident working a qualifying job collects an additional $10,000/month in Primary Job Subsidy plus market wages, placing the working floor at $240,000–$300,000 annually before any entrepreneurial income. Sanctuary residents routinely cross into Main Layer for commerce, dining, and cultural life, and the border between the two is fluid. Moving from Sanctuary to Main changes the trust infrastructure and leaves income the same. Full economic structure on the Systems page.
  • Enforcement Model: Post-Intervention. Implants offer failsafe motor inhibition, which citizens can disable. If a harmful act completes, victims are restored and perpetrators face immediate layer reassignment based on act severity. Full implant capabilities on the Technologies page.
  • Freedom & Autonomy: Full personal, creative, relational, and philosophical freedom. Consensual activities including combat sports, adult entertainment, substance use, and all forms of speech are unrestricted unless they cross defined harm thresholds. No preemptive censorship. No thought monitoring.
  • Population: The most populated and most diverse ring in the civilization: approximately 3 billion residents, with the density, energy, and variety of a civilization at full human scale. Main Layer is demographically mixed, with at least four distinct resident cohorts living side by side: native Main residents (ascended from autoparenting or born here, never reached Sanctuary) at roughly 2 billion; Sanctuary-credentialed elective residents (qualified for Sanctuary, retain full Sanctuary standing and SAD memberships, prefer Main’s scale and dynamism) at roughly 1 billion, or approximately 33% of Main’s population; phased-back Sanctuary residents who re-enter without stigma; and lower-layer-born children who relocated under Article VIII, building toward Sanctuary. Main Layer contains the full STI spectrum, from 0 to 100. A resident at 95 who qualifies for Sanctuary but prefers Main’s freedom lives in the same district as a resident at 15 who has never crossed a legal threshold serious enough to trigger reassignment. Both are legitimate Main Layer citizens, and that breadth is the texture of a civilization proving itself.
  • Infrastructure: Urban and suburban environments with high-technology service layers. Automated food fabrication, medical response, transit, and education. Neural diving hubs, augmentation clinics offering previews and first-tier modifications, and public forums are standard across all districts.

Demographic Composition and Credential Density

Main Layer’s single label hides a more complex demographic reality. Roughly 2 billion of its 3 billion residents are native Main: the majority cohort, at Main Layer’s baseline STI distribution, and the layer’s primary population in Main’s daily economic, cultural, and civic life. The remaining ~1 billion are Sanctuary-credentialed elective residents who qualified for +1 Sanctuary and currently choose to live in Main. Most are at life stages where Main’s scale, family proximity, commercial opportunity, and cultural dynamism fit their current priorities; many will move to Sanctuary residency at later life stages, when the layer’s curated character becomes more valuable to them. The Charter’s elective residency framework keeps Sanctuary residency available to them at any time, so the current composition is a snapshot of a lifecycle distribution rather than a fixed demographic. They carry the full portable credential architecture their Sanctuary standing produces: the 85+ STI verified continuously, SAD memberships earned over years or decades of sustained performance on specific measurable criteria, and the state-audited attribute claims those memberships represent. These credentials travel with them to Main. SAD memberships lapse only under punitive reassignment, never because a resident leaves Sanctuary by elective choice, so elective residency preserves the entire stack.

The result is a credential density differential across Main’s districts. An elite residential neighborhood in a cultural or commercial center might run at 60% or higher Sanctuary-credentialed density, well above Main’s 33% average, because such districts attract residents whose credential stacks give them economic and social leverage, and those residents cluster accordingly. An industrial district or a lower-income residential area might run at under 10% Sanctuary-credentialed density, with native Main residents dominating. The density varies continuously across the layer’s geography, producing an intra-Main stratification that the single layer label does not capture.

The credential leverage operates at the level of the specific stack more than base Sanctuary standing. Roughly 1 in 3 Main residents hold Sanctuary standing, so on its own it is meaningful but not a striking distinction. Distinction comes from specific SAD stack composition. A Sanctuary resident holding Relational Integrity Layer, Intelligence Standards Domain at the 145+ threshold, Physique Standards Domain at the 20% body-fat tier, Creative Output Domain, and Lineage Integrity Domain carries a stack that is rare even among Sanctuary-credentialed residents. That rarity is the actual currency across Main’s matching markets: dating, employment, partnership, residential community selection, mentor pairing, business collaboration. The architecture makes stacks portable and residents build their own compositions, while Main’s ambient credential density, lower than Sanctuary’s, lets rare stacks stand out.

Ascension and descent in Main are often invisible at the district level. A native Main resident whose sustained conduct eventually produces Sanctuary-eligible STI can choose to stay in Main rather than physically relocate; their credentials expand while their surroundings stay the same. A Sanctuary elective resident whose STI falls below 85 phases back to baseline Main standing without moving, losing the Sanctuary credential but keeping the same address. Neighbors may or may not immediately register either change, though the social change is real. Residents read each other as much through credentials as through shared geography, and because the credentials are state-verified and publicly readable, that reading is architecturally coherent rather than speculative.

A Day in the Life of a Main Layer Resident

Doctrine Snapshot: pre-v5.0

Dario Vance has been in Main Layer for three years. He arrived from Earth at 27, took the voluntary implant procedure on day two, and has worked since then as an urban systems designer, one of the roles that qualifies for the Primary Job Subsidy. His STI is 79. He is not trying to ascend. He came to VMSS to live well, and that is what he is doing.

He wakes in a well-proportioned apartment in the mid-ring district, not the most architecturally refined area of Main Layer but comfortable in the way real abundance makes things comfortable. Dashboard on the wall: UBI and subsidy both credited overnight, STI steady, no threshold events logged. He fabricates coffee and reads the morning civil digest, a feed of STI-notable events across the ring, public ledger updates, and community forum highlights. It lists a trust dispute between two neighbors four blocks over. He notes it and moves on.

His morning is site design work, reviewing schematics for a new transit node in the outer residential districts. He does three hours of focused output, then eats lunch with a colleague at a public courtyard. The food is free and the conversation easy. His colleague's STI halo shows green, and so does Dario's. At 79 they are both in the comfortable middle band of Main Layer social trust: no friction, no particular distinction.

Afternoon: an augmentation preview appointment, an audience-mode neural dive into a consenting resident currently running a transrace modification. He has been curious about the procedure for six months and wants to understand the subjective experience before deciding. He spends forty minutes in the dive and comes out thoughtful. He books a consultation for next week.

Evening: dinner at a neighbor's apartment, six people with mixed backgrounds and STI scores. One guest has a score in the high fifties, still green-band but visibly lower. The room shifts slightly around that fact without anyone naming it, and Dario has been here long enough to notice. After dinner he walks home through a quiet district. He knows, in the background, that this ring is not perfectly safe. Two people were reassigned from his district last month, one for fraud escalation and one for assault; the system logged and removed both within hours. Most of the time he does not think about it.

He goes to bed in a layer where safety is a live variable rather than a structural guarantee, which is how Main Layer was designed.

Enforcement in Practice

When a serious harmful act (murder, rape, severe assault) occurs in Main Layer, the implant's full recording system activates. The incident is captured via implant telemetry, AR environmental sensors, and AI reconstruction from multiple vantage points. A murder victim is revived via backup vessel in the Main Layer medical facility at full fidelity. Non-lethal harm victims receive immediate automated therapy and physical restoration. The perpetrator is reassigned downward within seconds of the act completing: to -3 Terminal for murder and capital violence, to -2 for severe but non-lethal predatory acts.

The system does not deliberate; the implant record is irrefutable. Consequence is immediate and permanent.

Edge Cases & Unique Aspects

  • Failsafe Disabling: Citizens may legally disable the implant's motor inhibition failsafe. If they subsequently commit harm, the act may complete, and consequence follows regardless. Disabling the failsafe removes the last structural barrier before accountability and leaves the accountability in place.
  • Practical Safety & Failsafe Adoption: Main Layer is classified as post-intervention: harmful acts can complete. In practice, the lived safety experience approaches Sanctuary for the overwhelming majority of residents. The failsafe is a free, invisible safety feature that costs nothing to leave enabled. It activates only when a resident is about to commit harm, so a resident with no intention of harming anyone never feels it. It is a seatbelt: technically optional, functionally universal among rational actors. The population that actively disables the failsafe is vanishingly small, because the only reason to disable it is to preserve the option of following through on something the system would stop. Main Layer's danger is real but statistical: a resident is far more likely to live their entire multi-century lifespan without encountering a completed harmful act than to witness one. The doctrinal distinction between Main Layer and Sanctuary is the possibility of encountering someone who opted out. Sanctuary eliminates that possibility entirely, while Main Layer lives with a margin so thin most residents never encounter it.
  • Downward Visitation & Elective Residency: Residents may visit or elect residency in lower layers for research, observation, or other reasons. Visitors and elective residents can return to Main Layer unless they commit a breach, in which case standard severity-based reassignment applies: the offense determines the destination layer, whatever layer is being visited or resided in.
  • Children Born in Lower Layers: Retain a standing right to relocate to Main Layer (0) at any age, enforceable without parental consent.
  • Cross-Layer Relationships: Routine and complex. Communication between residents in different layers is possible but subject to content mediation. Family members distributed across layers is common.
  • Revival Reliability: Revival in Main Layer is full fidelity — identity, memory, and personality are restored completely. The risk of revival failure is documented and disclosed at implant consent.
  • STI Visibility: All residents' STI indicators are visible in public interaction contexts. High STI broadens social and professional access; low STI creates friction and limits trust-gated opportunities without triggering enforcement.
  • Entertainment & Lifestyle Infrastructure: Main Layer is where the full breadth of VMSS lifestyle infrastructure operates. ImmersionTube, full sensory media, is a mainstream platform. Neural diving VR provides fully immersive virtual worlds without hardware. Food synthesizers produce any cuisine on demand. Bioengineered companions and body modification as fashion are culturally visible. Gaming, extreme sports (with backup vessel revival), and sensory art are major cultural institutions. Historical lifestyle communities recreate past eras with year-3000 safety infrastructure invisible underneath. Full detail in the World Dossier.
  • Metric Gated Domains (MGDs): Main Layer hosts the broadest and most varied population of MGDs in the civilization. Private, self-organized communities that admit members on any transparent measurable criterion: professional guilds, credential-gated residential blocks, discipline-specific collectives, sport leagues gating on rank, hobbyist circles gating on demonstrated output, and thousands of smaller domains built around whatever their founders cared to measure. MGDs are the layer's civic connective tissue, filling the space between state infrastructure and private life. They do not affect STI or layer status; exclusion from an MGD carries no VMSS consequence beyond loss of access to that specific domain. Federal floor law binds inside every MGD.
  • Regulatory Governance: Main Layer is the primary arena for the petition-based regulatory mechanism (Article XXVIII). With approximately 3 billion residents, the 1% signature threshold requires roughly 30 million signatures to surface a layer-wide regulatory issue: high enough to filter noise, low enough that genuine civilizational concerns reach the mechanism. Expert panels draft the regulation; 80% direct population ratification enacts it. Districts of one million residents are redrawn annually using real-time implant population data. District-level regulations address localized issues (wildlife management, infrastructure standards, resource allocation) within the framework set by layer-wide law. Main Layer generates the most regulatory activity of any layer because it has the largest population, the most diverse economic activity, and the broadest range of daily-life situations the charter and federal law were never designed to address.

Alternative Story — The Explorer

Simulation Type: Philosophical Journey · Classification: Conscious Homecoming · Outcome: Voluntary Return to Main Layer — Chosen by Conviction

Doctrine Snapshot: v9.0

Darius Okafor is 38 years old and has lived in Main Layer his entire life in VMSS. He arrived from Earth mid-tier, a former urban planner from Lagos: practical-minded, analytically sharp, comfortable with complexity. His STI settled at 76 within his first year, a solid, unspectacular score, the kind the system reads as stable civic baseline and files without comment.

He has thought about ascending for as long as he can remember being here, though never obsessively and never with the grinding hunger some residents bring to the ascension path. He is curious about what is on the other side, and quietly unsure whether he actually wants it.

In his sixth year he starts a civic architecture study of how institutional infrastructure shapes daily social behavior across all five layers. His STI climbs with the work, two or three points a year. In his tenth year it crosses 85, and he phases up to +1 Sanctuary automatically.

He arrives in Sanctuary on a Tuesday morning. The first thing he notices is the quiet, a specific absence of background vigilance he had not known he was carrying until it was gone. In Main Layer he scans without thinking: exits, faces, the subtle social calculus of shared space with people whose intentions are unverified. In Sanctuary that reflex has nothing to feed on. Every STI indicator around him is deep green.

By his third week he understands why people spend years working toward this. The conversations go deeper than Main Layer usually allows, and his own thinking sharpens without the friction. In his second month Ilse, a Sanctuary resident he has been collaborating with on the study, sets down her cup and asks him, "So are you staying?"

Sitting with the question, he realizes that he does not want to.

Sanctuary is everything it claims to be. Still, something in him requires the proving ground to remain real. The stakes in Main Layer are what make the choices matter: there nothing protects him from himself, and the freedom is costly because the consequences are real.

The study takes him to -1 in his fifth month. The contrast is immediate and instructive: more amber STI indicators in public spaces, private enterprise filling gaps the system has vacated, social life that takes more active navigation. He documents how residents organize and how private institutions emerge. He finds people living full lives under reduced privilege, people who adapted, built, and kept going.

-2 is harder. The enforcement posture is visible in thinning institutional infrastructure and the private order that has formed to fill the void. The social environment is more volatile. He does his work carefully and does not pretend it is comfortable. Even here he finds complexity where he expected flatness.

He returns to Main Layer on a Thursday afternoon. The scale hits him before he has fully processed the move. After months in Sanctuary's low-density trust environment and -1's frontier sparseness, Main Layer feels like surfacing into noise, and he means that as a compliment. Billions of people move through the same space at different points on the same journey: ascending, descending, holding steady, trying, failing, trying again. Every STI indicator in the crowd tells a different story.

He grew up in Lagos, and he knows instinctively that a city at full human density owes its quality of life to its friction. Sanctuary has 300 million residents, selective by any measure, but the SADs break it further into something closer to a constellation of small towns, each domain intimate enough that faces become familiar within weeks. The Cognitive Clarity Domain had the social texture of a university town.

Main Layer has billions, and its architecture, culture, and social texture reflect that in every interaction. A curated trust environment cannot replicate it, and a frontier settlement cannot manufacture it. It exists only where consequence, density, and human variety collide at civilizational scale.

Sanctuary was extraordinary. Main Layer is alive.

He elects Main Layer residency the following morning, and the system processes it in seconds. No notation in his file marks the decision as unusual; billions of residents choose Main Layer every day. His choice feels different to him anyway: he has seen the alternative clearly.

Key lesson: For some citizens Main Layer is the destination, chosen consciously after seeing what lies above and below. The proving ground is a consolation prize only to those who never asked whether the proof itself was the point. Nowhere else in the civilization has anyone built anything at this scale.
