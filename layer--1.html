-1 Noncompliance

Civic Violation Tier  ·  Post-Intervention Enforcement

-1 Noncompliance is the first layer of consequence in The Five Rings. Basic needs are still met, the system still functions, and residents retain most of their autonomy. Institutional abundance contracts, trust thins, and the social environment reflects the record that brought a person here. A functioning private sector fills the space that institutions withdraw from: market districts, repair cooperatives, freight operations, barter exchanges, and a commercial culture that runs on reputation because institutional mediation has stepped back. Penalized residents live alongside voluntary visitors from Main Layer and above, who cross into -1 for economic opportunity, personal ties, or curiosity, and alongside elective residents who chose -1 deliberately for its lower regulation and frontier economics. Much of the layer's social rhythm is set by commerce rather than by consequence.

Current tax frame: LP-074’s active 50 / 25 / 12.5 / 6.25 schedule sets -1’s top marginal rate at 25% above the unchanged $10 million threshold. Both schedules certified in 2294 and took effect in 2295. SCM scope remains unchanged.

Overview & Purpose

VMSS distinguishes between non-trivial violations and predatory harm. Chronic deception, harassment, fraud, and repeated antisocial behavior warrant consequence but do not belong in the same tier as predatory violence. -1 holds that distinction structurally. Residents here have shown conduct that makes them a net negative on trust in the environments above, though not a predatory threat to others.

The threshold into -1 is categorical: either a single qualifying event of sufficient severity (conduct that would result in incarceration: a DUI, an assault, meaningful fraud) or a sustained pattern of accumulation without corrective signal. Minor infractions in Main Layer are clearable and amendable, and the STI recovery mechanism allows behavioral trajectories to attenuate over time, so a pattern that trends poorly but self-corrects will not reach the reassignment threshold. The path to -1 requires harmful conduct sustained despite warnings, failsafe availability, and a measurable recovery window that the resident chose not to use.

A consequence in -1 does not mean a life of suffering. The layer holds approximately 600 million residents, and a citizen with a 500-year lifespan will not meet 1% of them. The social, economic, and cultural range available within -1 alone is broader than most Earth civilizations have ever produced, and -1 residents retain access to the two layers below them. In -2 and -3, a -1 resident's behavioral profile compares favorably with the local population's, so a -1 resident's accessible world spans three rings of civilization. The environment is rougher than Main Layer, the institutional presence is reduced, and the social texture reflects the population's behavioral history. The consequence reshapes the relationships, commerce, culture and centuries of experience ahead of a resident without diminishing their scale.

The economic draw that pulls voluntary residents into -1 shows in the numbers. The $5,000/month UBI floor goes further on a purchasing power gradient that runs 1.3–1.8x Main. The 25% top marginal tax rate is the first lower step in LP-074’s exact cascade. Speculative markets are legal: -1 is the first layer descending from Main that permits them. The regulatory overlay in commerce is generally thinner, while charter-floor enforcement on physical safety stays in full.

Key Characteristics

  • Economy: Reduced UBI plus Primary Job Subsidy for qualifying work. Layer-specific Compliance Token currency. It covers basic needs, not the comfort and access Main Layer provides. Fabricators are limited; in many districts goods require barter or direct labor exchange. The purchasing power gradient in -1 runs approximately 1.3–1.8x Main: each compliance token stretches further than a Main Layer dollar because the money supply is smaller, the population is roughly a fifth of Main's, and competition for basic goods is less intense. The gradient is narrowest here because -1's economy most closely resembles Main's in structure. Tourism from upper layers creates moderate inflationary pressure in commercial districts, pushing the gradient toward the lower end of the range in high-traffic areas.
  • Enforcement Model: Post-Intervention, the same framework as Main Layer. Harmful acts may complete if warnings are overridden; victims are restored, and perpetrators risk further reassignment to -2 or -3 for escalating conduct.
  • Environment: Functional but compressed. Buildings reflect lower maintenance cycles, public spaces are smaller and more contested, and lighting is noticeably dimmer than in Main Layer. Like the reduced resources, the physical environment tells residents that the civilization concentrates its investment elsewhere.
  • Monitoring: Logging-only AI tracking with no preemptive intervention. Drone coverage exists but response times are slower than in Main Layer. STI indicators across the population are predominantly orange and red, which shapes the social texture of daily interaction.
  • Population: The penalized population consists of residents assigned here for chronic low-harm violations: fraud, harassment, compulsive deception, repeated antisocial escalation. -1 also takes in people who were never penalized. Voluntary visitors from Main Layer and above enter regularly for economic opportunity, short-term contracts, or personal reasons, and voluntary permanent residents who chose -1 for its lower regulatory overhead and frontier market character form a meaningful segment of the community. Penalized and non-penalized residents coexist, and in daily commerce the distinction matters less than reputation does. Among the penalized, some are building economic footholds and improving their standing within the layer; others have settled into its rhythms without much orientation in either direction. Placement is permanent. STI improvement determines quality of life within -1; it does not restore eligibility for return.
  • Metric Gated Domains (MGDs): -1 hosts a substantial MGD population built around reputation-based commerce and private-order infrastructure. Trade networks admit on verified clean-conduct-since-descent records. Repair cooperatives gate on demonstrated skill and delivery consistency. Reputation-gated residential blocks require sustained STI improvement within the tier. Private security cooperatives admit on clean behavioral history inside -1, independent of the record that brought a resident here. MGDs in -1 are how the layer's private economy stratifies itself: reputation travels with every resident via the public ledger, and MGDs turn it into admission to better districts, better contracts, and better peer groups. Federal floor law still binds inside every -1 MGD.

A Day in the Life of a -1 Resident

Doctrine Snapshot: pre-v5.0

Petra Morrow is 31. She was reassigned to -1 fourteen months ago after a documented pattern of financial fraud: she manipulated contractor agreements across multiple projects for two years in Main Layer before the system accumulated enough logged intent to trigger reassignment. She knew it was coming. The implant had been warning her for six months, and she kept going.

She wakes in a single-room unit on the fourth floor of a mid-district residential block. There is no smart glass and no fabrication terminal, only a standard slot that dispenses a daily ration pack. Grey light comes in through a narrow window facing another building. She checks her dashboard: $5,000 Compliance Token credit, STI 38, no threshold events overnight. Her last threshold event was two months ago. In this layer only sustained clean conduct moves STI, and STI determines access to better districts, better contracts, and better standing among peers.

Her morning is repair labor. She contracted with a district maintenance cooperative three weeks ago and shows up at 8:00 for a four-hour shift fixing water recycling equipment in the residential blocks. The cooperative is one of dozens across the district (plumbing, electrical, fabricator maintenance, structural repair) that make up the self-organizing service economy filling the gap left by reduced institutional coverage. The work is unglamorous. The pay is 400 Compliance Tokens per shift, which supplements her UBI to a level that covers her costs with little remaining. She works alongside two other residents, both of whom descended for similar non-violent violations. On breaks the talk is mostly practical, occasionally about Main Layer. No one mentions it as somewhere they expect to see again.

Midday: she eats her ration pack on a public bench. Nearby the district market is running, a commercial strip of several dozen stalls and storefronts: a tool vendor run by a voluntary visitor from Main Layer on a six-month contract, a food cooperative that a resident of four years in -1 has built into a stable operation, a repair-parts exchange staffed by two people she recognizes from the residential blocks. The market is legitimate and is the district's primary economy. She stays out of it. She has learned to treat every discretionary choice in -1 as a calculation: participating means more interaction surface and more opportunity for the implant to log intent events, and she is trying to narrow her footprint.

Afternoon: she spends two hours at the district's neural access terminal. It is bandwidth-limited and Pilot Mode is unavailable, but Audience Mode still works. She enters a public experiential archive and spends the time on observation-mode dives through various Main Layer environments. The access is deliberately preserved in -1 as a mirror of what a different record produces. She has never found it cruel. It strikes her as accurate.

Evening: back in her unit, she reads and reviews her STI trend data, noting the small positive movement of the past two months. Placement is permanent, so the improvement won't take her anywhere else, but it opens doors within the layer: better cooperative contracts, access to higher-trust districts, fewer interactions with the predatory end of the population. She has adapted to the layer's pace, and sleep comes without much difficulty. She has not yet decided what kind of life she will build here.

Enforcement in Practice

Post-intervention applies in -1. Serious harm follows the same response logic as in Main Layer: victim restoration, perpetrator reassignment. The distinction in -1 is that escalation carries steeper consequences. A single act of violence in this layer moves a resident toward -2 directly, because the system treats the behavioral record that placed them in -1 as context for the current threshold evaluation.

Private detention exists in -1, but it operates within the charter's institutional constraints, which do not apply in -2 or -3. Article XIV's proportionality framework evaluates every consequence imposed, including those administered by private actors. Prisons in -1 may restrict privileges, impose basic rations, limit movement, apply short-term solitary confinement, and structure work-for-privileges programs. They may not do what -2 permits: indefinite solitary, sustained corporal punishment, nonlethal torment, or forced revival to deny the bailout escape. The system that evaluates residents also evaluates guards, so staff who cross those lines build a pattern on their own ledger. The AI governance infrastructure is still present, logging and running threshold checks. Private courts in -1 cannot impose disproportionate sentences either: a crony court that sentences a citizen to 300 years for a minor offense fails the proportionality check at the system level, and because VMSS is still the institutional backbone, the sentence is unenforceable. The Balanced Layer (the informal name for -1) earns its name by constraining both directions: residents cannot escape consequence, and the powerful cannot weaponize it.

In practice this runs on Article XIV's three-axis framework: severity, pattern, reversibility. Every act in -1 is evaluated against all three. A bar fight is moderate in severity, isolated and reversible: one axis, corrective intervention, STI hit. A loan shark running a sustained coercion operation is high severity and patterned, with compounding damage: three axes, qualifying event, -2 reassignment. A parent coercing a child to prevent them from exercising their constitutional right to relocate to Main Layer is high severity (denial of a charter right to a minor), sustained by definition, with compounding developmental damage: two to three axes, formal evaluation, likely -2. The system reads the axis profile and produces the output without subjective weighing. Because every actor's conduct, institutional or individual, is measured by the same instrument, the framework that protects residents from crony courts protects them from every other form of disproportionate harm.

Edge Cases & Unique Aspects

  • Permanence: Punitive reassignment to -1 is permanent, and the consequence is the environment itself. There is no appeal process, no fixed rehabilitation timeline, and no pathway back to Main Layer. STI improvement within -1 counts only inside the layer, where it determines access to better districts, local economic opportunities, and social standing among peers. It cannot restore eligibility for upward movement.
  • Voluntary Visitors: Main Layer residents who visit -1 can return unless they commit a breach while present, in which case standard severity-based reassignment applies. The boundary deters recklessness and leaves curiosity alone.
  • Children: Children born in -1 retain a standing right to relocate to Main Layer (0) at any age, enforceable without parental consent. The system does not proactively inform children of this right; discovery is organic. A parent who coerces a child into staying is subject to the three-axis evaluation described above. In -2 and -3, such coercion is logged on the parent's ledger but the community decides the response. In -1, the institutional presence catches it.
  • Revival After Death: Residents who die in -1 are revived at full fidelity via VMSS-operated fabrication proxy installation within the layer. Revival preserves continuity, and layer status and prior record carry over unchanged.
  • Bailout: A planned death. A resident in unbearable circumstances (sustained torture, extreme coercion, a situation with no viable exit) may choose to die deliberately, triggering backup vessel revival and resetting their immediate physical circumstances within the layer. Bailout is a continuity escape valve. It does not alter layer status, STI, or prior record, so it offers no escape from consequence. The option exists because VMSS is not designed to trap residents in suffering without recourse.
  • STI Visibility Dynamics: In a population where most indicators are red, high-trust individuals become socially conspicuous and are sometimes targeted for exploitation. The density of low-trust actors in -1 makes a clean record harder to maintain.
  • Regulatory Governance: The petition-based regulatory mechanism (Article XXVIII) operates in -1 with full institutional enforcement. The 1% signature threshold scales to -1's population. Expert panels draft regulations, and 80% direct ratification enacts them. Districts of one million residents are redrawn annually. In -1, regulatory law intersects with the layer's reputation-driven commercial culture: regulations governing trade disputes, cooperative standards, market rules, and infrastructure maintenance formalize what the private sector was already mediating informally. The AI governance system enforces enacted regulations through the same implant-ledger infrastructure that handles charter and federal law. Regulatory activity in -1 tends toward practical economic governance, the rules a functioning commercial layer needs and the charter was never designed to specify.

-1 is a consequence state, not a failure state. Placement is permanent, but the consequence carries information, and what a resident does with it inside the layer is still their choice.

Alternative Story — Pragmatic Adaptation

Simulation Type: Resident Profile · Classification: Pragmatic Adaptation · Outcome: Stable, Prosperous Life in -1 — No Ascension Pursued

Doctrine Snapshot: v9.0

Rafael Souza is 44 years old and has not thought about ascending in three years. He arrived at that deliberately, the way he arrives at most decisions: by looking at the actual numbers and ignoring the narrative around them.

He descended to -1 six years ago following a contractual fraud violation, neither violent nor predatory: a business dispute that crossed a threshold the system defines precisely and enforces without negotiation. His STI dropped from 68 to 41 in a single logged event. Layer reassignment to -1 followed within hours. His assets were liquidated at market value, his half absorbed by the treasury, his former business partner's half returned in cash. The system processed all of it in the same flat, efficient language it uses for everything.

He arrived in -1 with his skills, his contacts, and the -1 currency equivalent of a modest restart. A former logistics coordinator from Main Layer with fifteen years of supply chain experience and a reputation, even a damaged one, that people in the know still recognized.

Rafael understood immediately that the gaps in institutional coverage that make the Balanced Layer harder to live in also make it commercially interesting: where the system has partially withdrawn, demand exists for private solutions. Within eight months he had built a small private freight operation connecting -1 districts that the automated logistics infrastructure only partially served.

By year two the operation employed thirty people. By year four it had expanded into three districts. The tax rate in -1, 25% at the highest bracket (which he had not yet reached), was meaningfully lower than the progressive structure he had operated under in Main Layer. The regulatory overhead was thinner. His employees on 20-hour qualifying schedules collected the primary job subsidy on top of their wages, unlocking real discretionary time alongside stable income.

His personal life settled in ways he had not expected. The social texture of -1 was different from Main Layer: public spaces required more vigilance, STI indicators varied more, and social interaction had a rawer quality that took more active navigation. He found it exhausting in his first year and unremarkable by his third.

He lives well, in a good apartment in one of the better districts, with a social circle built from six years of reliable dealing in an environment where reliability is scarce.

The edge case in his simulation is a conversation with his sister, who remained in Main Layer. She makes a cross-layer visit in his fourth year, arriving economically neutral and working a short contract with his freight operation to participate in the local economy. She asks him whether he wishes he could go back. His STI has recovered to 59. The trajectory won't change his placement, but it opens doors within the layer that matter to the business.

He shows her the books: the tax differential, the regulatory environment, the market gaps in -1 that are already crowded in Main Layer. He does not argue that -1 is better, only that for the business he has built, in the environment he built it in, the life he has here is not the punishment she imagines. She listens, and does not fully agree. At the end of her contract she returns to Main Layer.

He has written nothing philosophical about where he is. His personal archive holds a spreadsheet, updated quarterly, that models his projected wealth accumulation in -1 and tracks the market conditions for his business model. The numbers are good and getting better. So far the conclusion has not changed.

Key lesson: Ambition can survive descent. For some residents, the layer they land in is the one that best fits their skills, appetite for risk, and economic calculation, and the system records them as a stable profile.
