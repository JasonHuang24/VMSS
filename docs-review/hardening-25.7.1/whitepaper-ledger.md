## Item 18 (page-wide de-tic): scope and limits

- **"load-bearing"**: replaced at 30 sites with the plain meaning ("essential", "decisive", "central", "core", "critical", "needed", "material", "weightiest", "matters", "governs", "stands"), or dropped. Covered: §4.4, §5.4.1, §5.4.2, §6.5, §6.6, §7 (×2), §10.6.1, §10.7, §10.7.1 (×3), §12.1, §15 (implant), §17.3.1, §17 callout, §18.8, §19.11, §19 (LP-047.3), §20.x scope, §21.4, §22 (×3), §24 (1447), §26, §28.0 (×2), §28.2, and the glossary Stratification-Integrity entry.
- **"load-bearing" kept**, because each use works as a term rather than a tic:
  - "load-bearing categor(y/ies)" (§28.2, glossary headword, "Technology Threshold Crossing").
  - "load-bearing charter provisions/sections".
  - "load-bearing rather than cemented" (§3.7, §31, glossary Founding Core). §3.7 defines this contrast with "immutable".
  - "load-bearing function/capability" in §28.0 and in the §18.8 sentences that point to it. §28.0 names the Redundant Envelope Pattern in these words, and laws.html uses the same phrase.
  - Security-classification criteria ("load-bearing system(s)/protocols": the Top Secret tier definition, §8, glossary).
  - "load-bearing civilizational infrastructure" (§10.1, §26, Tier 0 entries), a phrase shared with world.html, systems.html and resources-source.
- **"architecture" → "system"/"design"/"VMSS"/"doctrine"**: applied only in sentences already being edited (§4 callout, §4.4, §6.5, §10.7.1, §12.5, §22, §22.6, §24, §26). The page has 175 uses and many are the defined term (Five Instruments architecture, silo architecture, consequence architecture). A page-wide sweep was not done.
- **Em-dashes**: converted to commas, colons, full stops or parentheses only in edited sentences (about 15). The page has about 728 em-dashes, many of them paired parentheticals. Rewriting them page-wide in a canon document under clarity-only rules was judged too risky for one pass. This part of item 18 is only partially applied.

## Judgment calls and deviations

- **Item 2**: the Schedule B gloss uses the source's defined term, "Schedule B conditions (B1–B6)", from path-2-certification-2294.html. B1–B3 cover routing, obligation ordering and keyed months, and B6 is an adoption, so "revenue-coverage tests" would have misdescribed them. The predecessor petition is named by its defined title, RATIFY-TAX-50.
- **Item 12**: charter.html also carries XXV.IV (Enforcement Escalation Ladder) and XXV.V (Military Capability Doctrine), so the inserted sentence says only that XXV.I–XXV.III state three prohibitions directly and XXV.VI authorizes the federal list. The repeated "through the Article XXV.VI ladder" in the preceding clause was cut; no check-canon pin or guard find-string covers it.
- **Item 17**: the §4.4 quoted back-reference to "not a hierarchy of suffering" was left alone, as instructed. The §4.4 restatement of "Complete on its own terms" was rewritten as "Each layer's civic package is whole…", which avoids implying layers are self-sufficient when the paragraph's point is that the state carries the floor at every layer.
- **Item 14**: the LP-070 name was added at its first citation inside §12.5, not at the page's first LP-070 mention (§12.1, "LP-070 remains the enacted standing future gate"). The §12.1 sentence is a check-canon pin and a guard-mutation find-string, so it stays frozen.
