## 1. Scores

- **Makes sense on first read: 5/10.** The basic shape comes through quickly: five rings, a trust score, permanent descent, revival bodies, UBI. But from §4 onward almost every paragraph leans on LP numbers, Charter article numbers and pages I can't see, and the tax, savings-mandate and TIP/failsafe passages need several readings.
- **Internally consistent: 6/10.** The numbers hold up well (populations add to 4.3B, the 1,900 federal-administration seats work out exactly, the UBI, overtime and token-conversion figures agree). But a dozen rules contradict each other, including the core rule for when reassignment happens.

## 2. What I took away (about 150 words)

The Five Rings (also called VMSS) is a single-landmass civilization of about 4.3 billion people, founded by treaty in 2026. It is split by 15 km walls into five concentric rings. +1 Sanctuary (STI 85+) stops harmful acts before they finish using brain implants (TIP). Main lets acts happen and punishes them afterwards. Wrongdoers are moved down permanently: fraud and minor offenses to -1, violence to -2, murder to -3. In -3 there is no revival and almost no state. People can also choose to live lower down. The implant records conduct. A 0–100 Social Trust Index decides moves between Sanctuary and Main, while a separate criminal log drives punishment. The AI applies settled rules automatically. A Meritboard ranks people by competence to fill offices, and a novelty-only Supreme Court handles new questions. There are no elections: laws come from citizen petitions ratified by supermajorities. Automation pays a UBI that halves with each ring down. Backup bodies revive the dead except in -3.

## 3. Contradictions

1. **Is it proposed or enacted?**
   - index [61]: "A proposed voluntary civilization"; index [69]/[11]: "The Five Rings is a proposed civilization model"; whitepaper [246]: "A hybrid institutional draft"; whitepaper [1709]: "VMSS is intended to function as the most comprehensive proposal ever published".
   - whitepaper [361]: "is an enacted civilization architecture"; whitepaper [1703]: "The Founding Treaty, signed March 29, 2026, is not a proposal awaiting approval. It is a constitutional instrument that has already been ratified."
   - *Governs?* Only partly addressed. Index [399] says the pages "speak from the early 24th century", but the index itself still says "proposed". The reader has to guess.

2. **When does reassignment qualify: all three axes, or one event?**
   - whitepaper [566]: "Three-axis violations (severe harm, established pattern, irreversible damage) constitute a qualifying event for layer reassignment." The glossary repeats this at [1990].
   - whitepaper [574]: "The threshold into -1 requires either a single qualifying event of sufficient severity (... driving under the influence, assault, meaningful fraud)". Also [446] (murder "triggers immediate -3 reassignment"), [776] (nuclear possession "triggers immediate -3 reassignment") and [1202] (attempted murder "minimum -2"). None of these needs the pattern axis.
   - *Governs?* No. Left to the reader.

3. **No single metric decides punishment, yet the child count and debt reassign automatically.**
   - whitepaper [536]: "No single metric ... shall unilaterally determine punitive layer assignment or descent into lower layers."
   - whitepaper [956] and [2055]: "6th — Nuclear consequence: Immediate bankruptcy, full STI flag, asset liquidation, -1 reassignment". Also [946]: "-1 reassignment orders issue if the debt is not satisfied".

   - *Governs?* No.

## 4. Confusions

- STI "seven weighted measurement domains" [411] against the seven Charter dimensions: explained by the crosswalk at [472]–[502].
- SADs [440], the Colosseum classification [448], the Article XI gauntlet [416–417] and the SCM [878]: each is used before it is defined, and each is defined later (§20, glossary [1776], §9, §12.5).

But [1093] says vessel technology was ~0% at founding and calls "the gap between the aspiration and the current state" "the civilization's largest leakage category". - The headline leakage table [1605] says ~25% in 2150, covering Sanctuary, Main and -1. That is higher than Main's own 2150 figure of ~20% [1626], even though Main holds about 77% of those three rings' population. How the headline is weighted is never explained.

## 5. Where a reader gives up or is misled, and three small fixes

**Most likely to give up:** §12.1–§12.6 ([878]–[937]). The tax trajectory and savings-mandate attribution passages pile up LP numbers, audit schedules and "do-nothing" rulings with no plain statement of what a resident pays. §17.1.6 [1074] and §15.4 [1001] are nearly as dense.

**Most likely to be misled:**
- The three-axis rule at [566]. It leads a reader to believe one-off acts can't cause reassignment, which the rest of the document contradicts.
- The opening frame ("proposed" against "already ratified"). A reader can't tell whether they are reading a design proposal or a description from inside the world.

**Three smallest changes that would help most:**
1. Put one sentence under the whitepaper title: "Written in-world, c. 2300: dated events up to then are history, and later dates are projections." Then change index [61] "A proposed voluntary civilization" to match.
2. Add one sentence to §6.1 [566]: "Some acts qualify on a single occurrence without the pattern axis: murder and other capital acts, the three Charter absolutes, and the -1 offenses listed in §6.3."
3. Gloss the first use of "LP-042" at [251]: "(LP = an enacted Law Polling entry; the designated sections are ...)". Also correct [893] to "a dollar that survives the retention schedule".
