## 1. Scores

- **Makes sense on first read: 5/10.** The core idea comes through quickly: five rings, conduct sorts you, an implant records everything, and you descend permanently. The index never explains the machinery the law pages lean on (STI dimensions vs domains, TIP vs failsafe, Path 2, SADs vs MGDs, "child-six"), and the 60 Founding Corpus entries are dense, single-paragraph walls.
- **Internally consistent: 8/10.** I checked about 80 dates, vote counts, "Nth failure" tallies, population figures and cross-references ("since 2306, LP-080…"), and nearly all agree. The real conflicts are few and specific: the count of Charter filings, what the STI weights attach to, the index's sedation stage, the order of authority, and the regulatory table format.

## 2. What I now understand (about 150 words)

VMSS is a proposed voluntary civilization of about 4.3 billion people, set in the early 24th century and founded in 2026. It is divided into five walled rings: +1 Sanctuary (high trust, mandatory pre-crime inhibition), 0 Main (free will, punishment after the act), and three punitive rings, -1 Noncompliance, -2 Violent Offense and -3 Terminal (no revival, minimal state presence). Every citizen carries a brain implant that logs conduct and feeds a Social Trust Index (STI). An AI system, not a judge, evaluates serious acts and reassigns offenders downward permanently. Backup "vessels" revive the dead, with reliability that worsens layer by layer. Everyone gets a $10,000/month dividend that is never taxed, and a savings-garnishing mandate keeps wealth from piling up. Law comes in four tiers: the Charter, federal law, layer-wide regulation and district regulation. Each passes through supermajority gates in the Meritboard, the Supreme Court and the populations. Sanctuary can block a Charter amendment by withholding consensus. The register records mostly careful refinements, and many failures.

## 3. Contradictions

**C1. How many Charter amendments have been filed.**
- laws [615]: "Eight Charter amendments have been filed; seven failed, and the eighth… is the tier’s only success."
- law-polling [489]: "four Main-Layer-sponsored amendment campaigns across the prior three decades were withdrawn by their drafters at the Sanctuary deliberation window… Withdrawn filings never conclude and therefore do not enter this register."
- Those four reached the Sanctuary window, so they were filed, and the count of filings is at least twelve. Partly signposted: law-polling [281] limits the register to filings "carried to a disposition". The Laws page still says "filed", so the reader has to work out the discrepancy alone.

**C2. What carries the STI weights: the seven constitutional dimensions, or the seven measurement domains.**
- laws [1726] (Social Trust Measurement Standard): "observes the constitutional dimensions through seven weighted operational measurement domains — at approximate weights including civic compliance at about 15 and crisis response at about 10."
- laws [2702] / law-polling [3043] (LP-026): "output audit of the relative weights among the seven STI dimensions… The seven dimensions themselves remain constitutional and are not adjustable through this mechanism — only the weighting among them."
- laws [2702] even sets out the dimension/domain distinction, then attaches the weights to the dimensions. Nothing on the site resolves this; the reader has to guess.

**C3. Whether physical intervention means a major violation has already happened.**
- index [270–271]: "Sedation / Restraint — Physical intervention means the subject has already committed a major violation."
- laws [1661] (Threshold Inhibition Protocol): "failsafe motor inhibition… then nano-release sedation, then ambient drone countermeasures… so the act halts before it completes," and the protocol is mandatory in Sanctuary.
- In Sanctuary, and for anyone who opts in, sedation happens before any violation. The index flow is not labelled as Main-only or post-intervention, so the reader has to guess.

**C4. Order of authority.**
- index [399]: "the Charter governs, then enacted law (an enacted Law Polling entry is the primary record and VMSS Laws consolidates it), then the whitepaper."
- laws [305] / law-polling [2750], [282] (LP-042): "Charter articles, whitepaper sections at specification weight, and federal statutes… constitute primary authority." The Code itself "counts as secondary authority."
- The index places the whitepaper below enacted law and places VMSS Laws with enacted law. LP-042 makes the whitepaper co-primary and VMSS Laws secondary. This matters most for the 60 Founding Corpus entries: their only "Source" is a whitepaper section (for example laws [1544]), and they have no register entry to fall back on. When such an entry and the whitepaper diverge, the two stated orders give opposite answers. Neither statement refers to the other, so the reader has to guess.

**C5. Regulatory tables promise two figures and show one.**
- law-polling [2670]: "Each ratification table gives two figures: the ratification vote, shown first, which alone decides passage against the 80% threshold; and support saturation."
- Nearly every table shows only saturation. For example, [2857] "Main Layer 65% support saturation" and [2945] "-1 Noncompliance 57% support saturation" sit under "Ratified", against an 80% threshold. LP-082 [3317–3321] shows only "met".
- A reader takes these numbers for vote shares, and those votes would have failed. The rule is stated but the data does not follow it. Left to the reader.

**Count: 5 contradictions.** Four are left entirely to the reader; one (C1) has a partial hint on the other page.

- **Criminal code across layers.** laws [1956]: "no layer permitted a different criminal code." laws [1384] (LP-035) speaks of acts "prohibited only under a higher layer's own law", and LP-019 gives per-layer penalties.
- **SADs, MGDs and state-chartered domains.** Their relationship is never laid out. laws [2474] implies SADs sit above Sanctuary ("returned to Sanctuary or to Main").

## 5. Where a careful reader gives up or is misled, and three small fixes

**Where they give up:** the Founding Corpus band of laws.txt (lines 628–1070). It holds 60 consecutive entries, each one dense paragraph, with no summary of what changes from layer to layer. The fiscal entries (LP-073/074/075, Path 2) are the hardest; they read as legal apparatus with no plain-language result.

**Where they are misled:**
- The regulatory saturation tables (C5) make passed rules look like failures.
- The index Justice Flow (C3) teaches that sedation means guilt, which is false for Sanctuary.

**Three smallest changes:**
1. **law-polling [2670]:** replace the two-figures sentence with a label such as "Tables show support saturation only; every rule listed as Ratified passed its 80% ratification vote." Alternatively, add the ratification vote to each table.
2. **index Justice Flow [170]:** add one sentence: "This flow shows Main Layer's post-intervention default; in Sanctuary, and for residents who opt in, the Threshold Inhibition Protocol sedates before the act completes."
3. **law-polling [281] / laws [1529]:** add one line: "Version numbers (v4.0, v9.0…), R-numbered rulings and links to simulation pages refer to this website's editorial history, not in-world events." This tells the reader which text is law, which is history and which is site apparatus.
