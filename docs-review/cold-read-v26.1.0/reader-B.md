## 1. Scores

- **Makes sense on first read: 6/10.** The big shape is easy to follow: five concentric rings, implant ledger, permanent descent, UBI that halves at each step down. Within a page or two I could explain it to someone. The detailed rules for *what actually sends you down* are where I lost my footing, and I was never sure whether I was reading a 2026 proposal or a record from the 24th century.
- **Internally consistent: 5/10.** Numbers, populations, dates and vote thresholds check out to a surprising degree (4.3B sums correctly, the token conversions match the midpoint rates, the 50% child-tax curve does exceed inflow at the fifth child). But the Charter disagrees with itself on the core reassignment trigger and on -3, and the World page contradicts the Charter's founding core on Main-layer intervention.

## 2. What I take away (about 150 words)

The Five Rings (VMSS) is a voluntary civilization founded by treaty in 2026 on land ceded from northern Canada. By the early 24th century it holds 4.3 billion people. Five concentric walled rings run from Sanctuary (+1, centre) through Main (0) to Noncompliance (-1), Violent Offense (-2) and Terminal (-3, outer edge). A brain implant records conduct. AI governance, not judges, sorts people. Serious harm sends you down permanently. Sanctuary is gained and lost through a trust score (STI ≥ 85) and is the only reversible move. In Sanctuary, harm is stopped mid-act. In Main it can complete, and victims are revived from backup vessels. In -3, nobody is revived and the state barely governs. Everyone gets UBI ($10k a month at the top, halving with each ring down). Each lower ring has its own currency that cannot be converted upward. Law is made by petition, expert drafting and supermajority vote, and a ranked Meritboard supplies the President and the courts. Outside, VMSS deters the world with implants, nanobots and soldiers who come back after being killed. Sanctuary holds opt-in niche "domains" (SADs).

## 5. Where a reader gives up or is misled, and three smallest fixes

The most likely failure point is the Charter's account of **what gets you reassigned**: Article I [166] against Article XIV [300], combined with Article XII [296] against Article XXVII [422]. This is the civilization's central mechanic, and a careful reader comes away believing two incompatible rules. The second is **Main-layer intervention**. world [1217] quietly cancels the Charter's founding line [155], so a reader who trusts the World page is misled about the one difference between Sanctuary and Main that the whole design rests on. The third is the frame: a reader cannot tell whether the Charter's dates and leakage claims are a 2026 projection or 24th-century fact, which also produces C9.

Three smallest changes:
1. **One sentence in Article XIV:** "An Article I single qualifying event is read as meeting the three-axis profile because [reason]; the axis test governs everything else." Alternatively, drop "assault" and "DUI" from Article I.
2. **One clause at world [1217]:** either delete "in Main the same protocol halts it by default under LP-062.2", or add "(an enacted exception to the Charter's post-intervention rule for Main; see LP-062.2)" so the reader knows it is a deliberate override and not an error.
3. **One label under the Charter title:** "Text as amended to the early 24th century; the leakage dates in Article XXIII are the 2026 founding projection (see Roadmap)." Then correct "approximately 25%" for the 21st century in [343] to match the roadmap's 90%-to-25% path.

Assumption: I treated index [399]'s order of authority as the site's only general tie-breaker. Where a disagreement lies within one page, or the ranking would pick the visibly wrong side, I counted it as left to the reader.
