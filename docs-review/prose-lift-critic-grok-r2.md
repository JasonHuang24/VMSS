Recurring habits

1. Mid-story rule briefings. Highest cost in The Gray Kilometers ("Joss knows how the gray works" through the reassignment sentence). Smaller in Where the Maps End, in the Persistent threats sentence Calla remembers while lying awake. This is where the prose stops and teaches.
2. Sentences balanced into a line worth quoting. "A death was final, hers or theirs." "For anything short of killing nothing follows."
3. Sorted inventories. The crew roster, Maren's later-life duties, "people who had never engaged with composed art of any kind." These read as assembled. They slow the line more than they confuse it.
4. A short abstraction after the concrete beat. "Taken their labor with them." The tester's "most complex thing," one sentence before the burnt sugar. Lowest cost. The picture has already landed.

Verdict

Draft 4 needed.

1. The Gray Kilometers. Rewrite the paragraph that begins "Joss knows how the gray works." Leave one concrete sentence for the murder rule. Put the identification threat back in the leader's mouth, next to "Kill a trader and we're murderers," in the physical terms draft 2 already had: walks, voices, half our faces.
2. Where the Maps End. At "Nobody was arrested," restore the fact that past the markers there is no arrest. Draft 2's clause "out here there was no such thing" was doing that in six words, and the creek scene is misleading without it.
