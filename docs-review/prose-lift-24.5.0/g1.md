**Conventions (same as the pilot and the 24.4.x groups)**
- **Metadata blocks.** Title, Type, Classification and Snapshot are unchanged. One Outcome line (Hilary Duff) is rewritten for a Charter conflict and flagged old → new. The other three Outcome lines already say what happened in plain terms and are unchanged.
- **Word counts.** A count covers the body plus the Key Lesson (label included), excluding metadata. A word is any whitespace-separated token that contains a letter, digit or `$`. Originals were counted straight from `simulations.html` the same way.
- **Ledgers.** One row per doctrinal or factual claim in the original, in the original's order. Claims from the original's lesson line start "KL:", and row O is the Outcome line.
- **Quote marks in ledgers.** Double-quoted fragments are verbatim from the rewrite (10 words or fewer, italic asterisks ignored, metadata block included). Single-quoted fragments are original wording that was cut. The claim column uses no double quotes.
- **Real people.** No direct quote is invented for Musk, Trump, Biden or Duff. The one line of direct speech the original gives Musk is now reported indirectly. Every line in quotation marks belongs to an invented minor character. No real-world claim about any of the four is added. The Duff story's partner and children stay unnamed, and the partner stays ungendered, as in the original.
- **Sweep rulings.** DS-31 (Steve Jobs) is not in this group, and no doctrine-sweep ruling names these four cards. None of the four carries a score band, a floor for staying in Main, or an ascension review, application or consultation. The sweep lists the Trump card among T2's "adjacent passages" (a single act from Main straight to −2) without a ruling, so the rewrite keeps the direct −2 reassignment as written.
- **Charter check.** Every VMSS-side statement was read against the current Charter. Three cards (Musk, Biden, Duff) have the character qualify for Selective Ascension Domains while in Main, with ascension following, which makes SAD entry the route to Sanctuary. Charter Art. IX defines SADs as domains "nested within Heaven Layers", and phasing up happens automatically at STI 85. Musk's custom-domain petition moves to after his phase-up as well, so he petitions inside his own layer (whitepaper: new SADs route through the Art. XXVIII petition). Visits run downward only (Biden's cross-layer mediation from Sanctuary). **Holds: none.**
- **Invented names** were checked against every `.html` and `.md` file in the repo, worktrees excluded. Nell Kasprow, Gilla Pask, Orla Teague, Hollis Rade, Selka Brun and Oona Belk appear nowhere else.

## 1. Elon Musk

**Whose story:** Elon Musk. The implant's limits reach him as engineering constraints on his first day, his STI carries him into Sanctuary within the year, and the one flag the implant raises on him ends up raising his score.

**Rewrite**

```text
Elon Musk
Simulation Type: Historical Personality · Classification: Adaptive Outcome · Doctrine Snapshot: pre-v5.0
Outcome: Highly Adaptive — Rapid Ascent to +1 Sanctuary
```

Elon Musk entered Main Layer as a voluntary opt-in. He was fifty-four, a detail he already planned to revise. Within hours of receiving his implant he had submitted a formal data request to the VMSS infrastructure team: he wanted to understand the Threshold Inhibition Protocol at the engineering level.

Nell Kasprow, the engineer who answered it, took him through what the implant read. "Cognition is non-public," she said. "Intent and execution are monitored."

He nodded and told her that was good. Those were measurable constraints, he said, and he could operate inside measurable constraints.

From that first day he treated VMSS as an optimization problem with hard physical boundaries. Because he had genuinely internalized its logic, his STI began rising immediately. He never tested a threshold, which in his framework was a waste of cycles. He modeled the ascension path and began executing against it.

Within three weeks he had assembled a team of twelve, none of them recruited through conventional channels; he had found them through public STI records and the competence evaluations available through the neural network. Before his first month was out they were running an open-source project to reduce backup vessel revival failure rates. The environment rewarded the way he had always worked, without the institutional friction he had spent decades navigating on Earth. He slept four hours, ate nutrient packs and worked eighteen-hour days.

His STI crossed 85 within the year, and he phased up to +1 Sanctuary. There he qualified for the Cognitive Clarity Domain and the Decision Velocity Domain, and successfully petitioned for a custom Interplanetary Infrastructure Domain he had proposed. His work expanded to long-horizon civilization architecture: interplanetary transit networks, Dyson-sphere feasibility modeling, backup vessel revival reliability. He became one of the most productive residents in the Sanctuary ring.

During a high-fidelity neural dive simulating a resource failure in a Mars colony, he briefly generated intent signals, and the implant flagged them immediately: *consistent with selective exclusion of low-performing participants*. He exited the simulation, reviewed the log, and rebuilt the scenario with different parameters.

His STI actually rose afterward. Gilla Pask, who shared the dive suite, showed him the reading.

"It weights self-correction under pressure," she said. "Positive conduct signal."

He found this amusing and noted it in his personal archive.

Key lesson: VMSS requires no productivity, and it was designed to concentrate resources around extreme ambition working inside a framework it genuinely respects. Such ambition becomes one of the most productive forces the civilization can generate.

## 2. Donald Trump

**Whose story:** Donald Trump. Every warning lands on his own implant, the rally sends him to −2, and the turning point is a district boss there asking who holds the street for him.

**Rewrite**

```text
Donald Trump
Simulation Type: Historical Personality · Classification: Incitement Risk · Doctrine Snapshot: pre-v5.0
Outcome: Unconstrained — Rapid Reassignment to -2 Violent Offense
```

Within hours of entering Main Layer, Donald Trump was posting on the public neural forum. The posts were characteristic: competitive framing, exclusionary rhetoric, a narrative of winners and losers with himself as the authoritative classifier of both. Within the first day the STI ledger was logging entries against the cumulative pattern of his expressed conduct.

His following grew quickly; speech was protected until it crossed defined thresholds. After a neural rally where he explicitly called for the exclusion of lower-ring residents from Main Layer social infrastructure, the implant issued its first formal warning, clear and specific. He dismissed it as a system error.

Orla Teague, who ran his channel, read it over his shoulder. "It quotes you," she said. "Word for word."

Over the following weeks his public communications escalated from competitive framing to explicit incitement: calls for coordinated exclusion, language framing violence against certain groups as a form of social hygiene. He overrode each escalating warning.

At a large organized rally the system detected intent plus imminent execution of coordinated incitement-to-harm, which crossed the criminal escalation threshold. Reassignment to -2 Violent Offense was immediate.

In -2, standing came from territorial credibility. Within days the gang leaders who controlled resources in his district had read him accurately. On the steps of her depot, Hollis Rade let him describe his following in Main.

"That's upstairs," she said. "Who holds this street for you?"

She told her lieutenant to keep him away from the depot. His influence contracted rapidly. He still narrated his situation as an injustice done to him, and the implant still logged. He never went back up.

Key lesson: Once rhetorical charisma organizes people toward harm, the system measures it as direct harm, whatever the framing.

## 3. Joe Biden

**Whose story:** Joe Biden. His score climbs on conduct nobody announces, and the doctrine reaches the page through the questions new arrivals bring him.

**Rewrite**

```text
Joe Biden
Simulation Type: Historical Personality · Classification: Institutional Stability · Doctrine Snapshot: pre-v5.0
Outcome: Aligned — Steady Ascent to +1 Sanctuary
```

Joe Biden entered Main Layer quietly. He did not announce himself or seek a platform, and he did not begin by positioning himself in relation to the system's hierarchy. His STI began rising before he had done anything notable, because the baseline conduct the system read from him was already scoring well: attentive, non-threatening, oriented toward engagement rather than domination. He spent his first week in orientation, reading the charter carefully, attending two public forums as a listener, and walking the district for several hours each day.

Within two weeks he had organized his first small community session, a practical one for new arrivals. The sheet by the door listed threshold logic, the STI system, and the mechanics of downward visitation, elective residency and voluntary permanent residency. He had no formal authority and sought none.

Afterward a woman stayed behind. "Which kind of residency can I take back?"

He told her elective residency, and promised her the charter's wording by Thursday. It arrived on Thursday.

Over the following year his work expanded without becoming institutional. He volunteered regularly in neural orientation dives, sharing his subjective experience of loss, grief and recovery in ways new residents found useful in calibrating to their own circumstances.

"Who did you lose?" Selka Brun asked him after one dive.

People asked him that often; in sessions he always turned it back to them. He asked about her father.

His STI climbed steadily until it crossed 85, and he phased up to +1 Sanctuary. There he qualified for the Relational Integrity Layer and the Emotional Regulation Domain, and continued the same work at a larger scale: cross-layer mediation for families separated by reassignment, mentorship for residents just starting the climb toward 85, and quiet advocacy within the Meritboard for clearer communication to new arrivals about threshold logic.

Key lesson: The contribution metric is designed to capture showing up, explaining clearly and following through. Consistent, unglamorous care for others' stability is one of the highest-performing civic profiles the system recognizes.
