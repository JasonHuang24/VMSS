**Conventions (same as the pilot and the 24.4.x groups)**
- **Metadata blocks.** Title, Type, Classification and Snapshot are unchanged. The Outcome lines are unchanged too. All five Historical Personality cards open their Outcome with an alignment tag ("Aligned —", "Contested —"), and each of these three already states what happened, so rewriting one would break the section's pattern for no gain.
- **Word counts.** A count covers the body plus the Key Lesson (label included), excluding metadata. A word is any whitespace-separated token that contains a letter, digit or `$`. The originals were counted from `simulations.html` the same way.
- **Length cap.** None of the three originals has section headers, so none is a case-study conversion and each is capped at its original length (the 24.4.3 reading). All three land 0.7–1.3% under, short of the guide's 10–25% aim. The originals are compressed profiles with few aphorisms to cut, and the two dialogue scenes the guide requires cost about what the cuts freed. Cutting further would mean dropping a ledgered fact or a scene. This could go either way.
- **Ledgers.** Each ledger is checked against the original, one row per doctrinal or factual claim. Row O is the Outcome line, and claims from the original's lesson box start `KL:`.
- **Quote marks in ledgers.** Double-quoted fragments are verbatim from the rewrite, metadata block included (10 words or fewer). Single-quoted fragments are original wording that was cut or changed. The claim column uses no double quotes.
- **Real people (standing ruling).** No rewrite gives any of the three a direct quote. What they say is reported ("He told her the interface had been bad."), and only invented minor characters speak in quotation marks. No real-world claim is added. The Earth-life sentences for Swift and Jobs and the twentieth-century clause for Mandela are the originals' own.
- **No score bands, no ascension applications (standing ruling, DS-31).** Applied to Steve Jobs (see its flags). In all three, the move up happens when the STI crosses the Sanctuary threshold. The number 85 is not written in, because none of the originals gives it.
- **Charter Art. IX fix (Swift and Jobs).** Both originals have the character qualify for SADs in Main and then ascend ('She qualifies for Relational Integrity Layer and Emotional Regulation Domain within three years. Ascension to +1 Sanctuary follows.' / 'He qualifies for Cognitive Clarity Domain. Ascension to +1 Sanctuary follows in his fifth year.'). Art. IX nests SADs 'within Heaven Layers', and `sads.html` calls each 'a voluntary, revocable domain within Sanctuary', so SAD qualification comes after the move up, and a SAD can't read as the route to Sanctuary. Both rewrites reorder the two events. This conflict is not in the sweep register. The sweep's DS-39 (Kai Lindström) raised the same Art. IX point as a story ruling.
- **'In Heaven' → "In Sanctuary" (all three).** Charter VI names the Heaven Layers as '+1 Sanctuary & SADs', and each original means the ring it names in its Outcome line. The rewrites use "Sanctuary" throughout.
- **Quote marks outside the ledgers.** In the conventions and flags, too, double quotes hold rewrite wording only. Original, canon and earlier-draft wording is in single quotes.
- **Invented names** (Wendell Rusk, Hana Kobel, Gideon Tull, Bettina Oduya, Sabeth Quist) were checked against every `.html` page at the repo root and every `docs-review` `.md` file, `.claude/worktrees` excluded. None is used there. No other 24.5.0 group file existed when this was written, so cross-group clashes are unchecked. The first-choice names (Nadia Ferro, Odile, Priya, Teodor, Vell) were already taken and were replaced.

## 1. Taylor Swift

**Whose story:** Taylor Swift. The 74-to-61 drop lands on her own log, and the choice not to contest it is hers. The orientation officer, Hana and Gideon Tull are invented minor characters around her.

**Rewrite**

```text
Taylor Swift
Simulation Type: Historical Personality · Classification: Cultural Architecture · Doctrine Snapshot: pre-v5.0
Outcome: Aligned — Ascent to +1 Sanctuary; Significant SAD Contributions
```

Taylor Swift arrived in Main Layer with extensive public recognition and a profile that immediately gave the system an unusual set of variables to evaluate. Within hours the forums were full of her. Her orientation officer, Wendell Rusk, had the threads open when she sat down.

"Celebrity isn't an STI input," he said, turning the screen toward her. "This just means more people who can endorse you. And more people watching."

On Earth she had lived in public, where image management was operationally necessary and relational transparency was a vulnerability. Here the implant measured expressed conduct and had no reading for managed presentation, and the public ledger logged relational breaches whether or not they reached conventional media. Her first months went to a deliberate recalibration, in earnest and not without friction. She caught herself drafting careful statements nobody had asked for, and deleted them. Her STI log that year was a steady learning curve with no clean ascension line.

In her second year a contractual dispute with a creative collaborator, Gideon Tull, ended in a logged trust breach, judged non-criminal and publicly visible. Her STI dropped from 74 to 61 in a single logged event, and the forums had it by evening.

"You can contest the record," said Hana Kobel, who engineered her sessions. "People do."

She didn't. That night she addressed the breach directly in a public forum post. The system evaluated the post as a high-quality accountability signal, and over the following months her STI recovered.

Her creative output was substantial: neural musical architectures, experiential works that let an audience inhabit an emotional state with a specificity conventional music cannot produce. The work was technically sophisticated and drew strong endorsement signals from a broad population. Before her third year was out her STI had crossed the Sanctuary threshold, and she had moved up to +1 Sanctuary and qualified for the Relational Integrity Layer and the Emotional Regulation Domain.

In Sanctuary she petitioned for a neural music SAD, a Selective Ascension Domain gated by demonstrated depth of musical engagement and production, and was a primary architect of its design. It drew several hundred residents in its first two years and became a recognized feature of the +1 creative landscape.

Hana sent her the Main Layer orientation program's syllabus. The dispute with Gideon Tull was one of its more-studied cases, her forum post attached.

Key lesson: A trust breach and the conduct after it are scored separately. Owning one publicly is good conduct in its own right.

## 2. Steve Jobs

**Whose story:** Steve Jobs. The logged violations and the low-to-mid-50s score are his, and so is the dive. Bettina Oduya, an invented collaborator, sees the ledger entry and hosts the dive, and she is the one who speaks.

**Rewrite**

```text
Steve Jobs
Simulation Type: Historical Personality · Classification: Conditional Ascent · Doctrine Snapshot: v8.0
Outcome: Contested — Prolonged Main Layer Tenure; Late Ascent
```

Steve Jobs entered Main Layer and decided at once that the system was the most interesting design problem he had ever encountered. It was the philosophy that drew him. A civilization built around legible consequences for behavior was, in his read, the kind of constraint that produces the best possible output. He respected the architecture, and immediately began looking for its edges.

His first year was about as problematic as it was productive. His creative and technical output was exceptional: he designed neural interface environments significantly cleaner and more intuitive than anything the system's default infrastructure offered, and donated them to the public commons without hesitation. His STI rose through contribution. It also fell, repeatedly, through conduct. His interpersonal behavior, historically characterized by extreme demands, public humiliation of collaborators and sustained manipulation framed as high standards, generated logged trust violations on a consistent basis.

One afternoon he took a junior designer's interface apart in front of the studio. That evening the entry was on his STI ledger, and he turned the display so a collaborator, Bettina Oduya, could see it.

"Full weight," she said. "Same as anyone's."

He told her the interface had been bad.

"It was," Bettina said, and closed the ledger.

For most of his first three years his STI sat in the low-to-mid 50s, short of the Sanctuary threshold. His entire Earth life he had operated inside systems where exceptional output granted relational latitude, and he found the 50s genuinely frustrating.

In his fourth year he and Bettina, by then one of his long-term collaborators, entered a voluntary neural dive.

"Audience mode," she said, settling into the chair. "You don't steer. You get what working alongside you has been like for me, all of it."

He did not discuss what he observed in the dive publicly. Over the following months the STI system recorded a consistent change in his interpersonal conduct pattern, incremental and sustained, with nothing dramatic in it. His STI climbed past 70, then past 80. In his fifth year it crossed the Sanctuary threshold, and he moved up to +1 Sanctuary, where he qualified for the Cognitive Clarity Domain.

In Sanctuary his creative output accelerated. He designed several SADs himself, rigorous, opinionated and beautifully constructed, and they became some of the most sought-after access environments in the ring. He never became easy to work with by conventional standards. He opened every working session, Bettina's included, by telling his collaborators exactly what he would demand of them.

Key lesson: Good work earns no exemption for how a person treats others. The two are assessed separately, and neither offsets the other.

## 3. Nelson Mandela

**Whose story:** Nelson Mandela. The rising score and the -3 argument are his. The back room of a transit depot, Sabeth Quist and the man at the next session are where the listening and the challenge happen.

**Rewrite**

```text
Nelson Mandela
Simulation Type: Historical Personality · Classification: Institutional Trust · Doctrine Snapshot: v8.0
Outcome: Aligned — Direct Ascent to +1 Sanctuary
```

Nelson Mandela read the charter in full on his first day in Main Layer. He read it as a philosophical document, tracing the logic of each article back to its foundational premise and noting where the system's conclusions aligned with principles he had spent decades working toward on Earth, and where they diverged. With the precision and intellectual honesty that had produced some of the most consequential institutional work of the twentieth century, he shared his opinions about both in the public forums.

His STI began rising before he had engaged in any formal civic activity. The system was reading a deeply embedded behavioral disposition in his public conduct, the kind the STI is built to measure precisely: intellectual honesty, a consistent acknowledgment of complexity, and a demonstrated pattern of treating adversaries as people rather than problems to be eliminated. Within his first month the score was in the high 70s.

He focused his early months in Main Layer on the residents VMSS tends to render invisible: low STI scores, not close to any threshold, settled into their circumstances without orientation in any particular direction. He held listening sessions. At the first, in the back room of a transit depot, a night cleaner named Sabeth Quist sat with her arms folded.

"Aren't you going to tell us something?" she said.

He asked her about her week instead, wrote down what she said, and asked again where he hadn't understood. His STI rose on endorsement signals from people like Sabeth, the least prominent residents in the ring.

He did not endorse the -3 Terminal layer without qualification, and in several carefully argued forum posts he challenged the finality of terminal placement.

"Those are dangerous people down there," a man said at the next depot session.

Mandela agreed that some were, and said again what the posts had been arguing all along: any civilizational system should examine the permanence of its most severe consequences over time. The STI read that engagement with the system's own design as a positive signal, since he conducted it honestly and without self-interest. The Meritboard engaged with his arguments seriously, and some of his proposals influenced subsequent charter review discussions.

He qualified for +1 Sanctuary within eighteen months, one of the faster ascensions on record for a new arrival without prior VMSS experience. In Sanctuary he became a significant figure in the civilization's long-term governance framework, contributing to the Meritboard's review processes and serving as one of the most cited voices in the ongoing development of the charter.

Key lesson: Holding VMSS to its own stated principles, honestly, specifically and without personal grievance, is among the highest civic contributions it is designed to recognize.
