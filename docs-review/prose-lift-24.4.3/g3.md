## 1. The Second Chance Tourist

**Whose story:** Marcus Hale, a Main Layer engineer on a one-year visit. The cooperative reads his record, puts him to work, offers him a permanent place, and he takes the finding home.

**Rewrite**

```text
The Second Chance Tourist
Simulation Type: Civilizational Scenario · Classification: Downward Visitation Research · Doctrine Snapshot: v17.9.4
Outcome: Extended -3 residency with clean ledger — permanent residency offered and declined; returned to Main Layer with a report
```

Marcus Hale was forty-two, a Main Layer materials engineer with a pristine record and an STI of 79, and tired of reading about -3 as if it were an exotic zoo exhibit. He wanted the unfiltered version, so he filed a one-year voluntary visitor application. The system approved it instantly: clean ledger, no flags. He arrived with nothing but curiosity and a notebook.

He found qualifying work as a structural assessor for a territorial mining cooperative. The foreman, Ottilie Hesse, read his Main Layer credentials.

"No permanent flags," she said. "So you're a tourist." She looked him over a while longer. "Call a pillar sound when it isn't, and no crew on this ridge hires you again. Start tomorrow."

Within weeks he was crawling through the tunnels, diagnosing stress fractures and marking each in chalk.

He watched private justice settle a water-rights dispute at the east spring in one afternoon. Two crews faced each other across the channel, and nobody sent for a lawyer or filled in a form. One boss read an entry aloud from a reputation ledger. The other boss listened, then his crew coiled their hoses and left. Nobody reopened it. Marcus wrote it all down, sitting on an ore cart.

Six months in, Ottilie found him at the pithead.

"The cooperative wants you permanent," she said. "File for permanent residency and the assessor's post is yours."

He said no, and she didn't argue. Tovan, a punitive arrival, had worked the deep shafts for years and never been offered a post.

When his year ended, Marcus went back to Main Layer and wrote a report saying -3 wasn't punishment for everyone, and that some people preferred it raw. He attached the notebook, chalk dust and all.

Key lesson: -3 judges people by how they arrived and how they behave. Voluntary visitors with clean records find it a frontier of opportunity.

**Flags**
- **Outcome line changed.** 'Extended -3 residency with clean ledger — educational immersion reveals the frontier as opportunity, not punishment' → 'Extended -3 residency with clean ledger — permanent residency offered and declined; returned to Main Layer with a report'. The first half is kept word for word. The second half was a reversal and is replaced with what happened.
- **Charter reading, the permanent-residency offer (row 23).** The original has the cooperative "offer him permanent residency". Under the current Charter a cooperative can't grant that: voluntary permanent residency is a formal filing, with psychological screening and liquidation under Art. III.V (Art. VII). The rewrite keeps the offer and routes it through the filing ("File for permanent residency and the assessor's post is yours."), so the cooperative offers the post and the filing stays his. The new Outcome's "permanent residency offered" reads as that invitation. This could go either way; the alternative is to keep the original's wording and let the cooperative's offer stand as loose speech.
- **"Residency" in the Outcome line.** Marcus is a visitor, and the current Charter uses "residency" for elective and permanent residency. The frozen half of the Outcome keeps "Extended -3 residency" as the original wrote it. Worth a look if the Outcome is revisited.
- **Invented texture:** Ottilie Hesse, the pillar warning and "Start tomorrow", the chalk, the east spring and the channel, the coiled hoses, the ore cart, the pithead, "she didn't argue", Tovan, and the notebook attached with its chalk dust.

## 2. The Immortal Influencer

**Whose story:** Jax Rivera, the streamer the exploit is named after. The locals turn him away at their gates, a petition names the loophole he is using, and the citation lands on his public ledger.

**Rewrite**

```text
The Immortal Influencer
Simulation Type: Civilizational Scenario · Classification: Disclosure-Era Cultural Exploit · Doctrine Snapshot: v17.9.4
Outcome: Federal legislative refinement — the disclosure-era cultural exploitation gap identified; escalation culminating in LP-004.2's terminal severance clause
```

Jax Rivera, a Main Layer content creator, had 42 million followers when he announced *Terminal Unfiltered*: a full voluntary year in -3 Terminal, livestreamed. The Lower-Layer Economic Disclosure Law (LP-006) had been federal law for nine years, and the parity redraft that would seal vessel links at the -3 boundary was a decade away. Jax did the bare minimum. Every stream opened with the legally required disclosure: "Main Layer. Backup vessel active." He kept out of dangerous labor and Colosseum bouts and just filmed.

In the first month he toured the wilder districts, interviewed territorial operators and filmed private justice proceedings for an audience that had never seen the raw frontier. Donations flooded in, and the clips spread. Without technically breaking a law, he became the most recognizable face in -3.

By month four the locals were done with him. Private security cooperatives began refusing him entry. At one gate the guard read his record, where the reputation-ledger networks had tagged him: *deathless tourist*.

"You film us getting through the day," she said, "so people upstairs have something to watch."

"I disclose. Every stream."

"I know. I've seen it." She waved the next vehicle through.

Around then he filmed a territorial dispute that escalated when one side started playing to the camera. Two native residents died on stream.

His -3-context STI tanked. He looked at it once, between streams. He was still a Main Layer citizen with full upward mobility, and the donation balance kept climbing.

In month seven a coalition of -3 cooperatives filed a fresh Article XXVIII petition. It granted that Jax was within the current law. It argued that cultural exploitation through livestreaming created the same economic and social distortion the Disclosure Law was supposed to surface, and that disclosure alone had a gaping loophole the drafters never saw coming.

"A loophole," Jax told the camera. "I'm a loophole."

By then the Meritboard's economics division had published the data, and Jax put it on screen for his chat: native dangerous-labor participation down another 11%, Colosseum attendance in collapse, and several territorial cooperatives struggling to keep order because residents were performing for the audience instead of negotiating.

Nine months after he arrived, the federal ladder was invoked again, and a new provision was drafted: Cultural Spectacle Exploitation. Any visitor with active continuity who generated significant upper-layer revenue or attention by turning -3 into content was now subject to the same disclosure-plus-exclusion rules already covering dangerous labor.

Jax was the first person cited under it. He was ordered to stop livestreaming from -3 or face federal consequences. He complied on the final day of his contract, went back to Main Layer and immediately released a new series, *How I Broke -3 (Legally)*.

His permanent public ledger note now carries the cultural exploitation citation next to his original disclosure record. Future -3 visitors see it immediately.

Key lesson: Federal law is revised as each new form of an exploit surfaces. This escalation fed the Backup Vessel Parity redraft (LP-004.2), whose terminal severance clause made deathless spectacle structurally impossible.

## 3. The Architect Who Built His Own Prison

**Whose story:** Elias Voss, a Sanctuary architect who files for -3 to build against resistance. The locals read the permanence on his record and take his generator, his people and his tribute.

**Rewrite**

```text
The Architect Who Built His Own Prison
Simulation Type: World Scenario · Classification: Formal Voluntary Permanent Residency / Creator's Hubris · Doctrine Snapshot: v17.9.8
Outcome: Self-Designed Compound in -3 — Still Standing, Paying Tribute to Three Cooperatives
```

Dr. Elias Voss, one of the most sought-after architects in Sanctuary, had designed half a dozen landmarks that made paradise look even more perfect on the brochure. At the opening of the sixth, a colleague found him staring at it.

"It's perfect," she said.

"It's too fucking easy."

He cashed out, took the brutal currency conversion hit, and filed for formal voluntary permanent residency in -3. He wanted to build something that could fight back.

He arrived with a fraction of his old wealth and all his ego. Six months later he had a fortress up: reinforced walls, private power, water systems, and automated defenses most -3 warlords would envy. He thought he was above the layer's organic order.

The first trader to read his record stopped at the flag. "Permanent residency," she told the man behind her.

The locals set about educating him. First came the "accidental" boundary tests, then the thefts. After they stole his backup generator, they left a polite note: *Welcome to -3, architect.*

By year two the compound still stood, and he was paying tribute to three different cooperatives. The private justice system he used to call primitive settled water disputes faster than any Sanctuary court could. His best people had been poached twice.

He doesn't miss Sanctuary. He is redrawing the east wall again.

Key lesson: Some creators choose -3 because only consequence makes their work feel real. The layer resists them without punishing them.
