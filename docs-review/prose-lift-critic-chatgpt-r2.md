# 6. Recurring habits

1. **Briefings disguised as viewpoint.** This is the largest remaining problem. Giving information to a character does not automatically make its presentation feel like that character’s experience. Calla’s crew classification and Joss’s town profiles, rules paragraph and almanac explanation retain the shape of inventories or reference material. The problem is concentrated in Piece 3; it is not an objection to the fixed vocabulary or to explanation itself. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="40"} :chatgpt-content-reference{index="41"}

2. **Restating information immediately after establishing it.** This remains visible in Piece 2’s repeated audience expectation and the dialogue about the unexplained smell. Both instances pause the forward movement to deliver information the reader already has. The second is more noticeable because it occupies a separate paragraph near the ending. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="42"} :chatgpt-content-reference{index="43"}

3. **Reaching for a stronger-sounding formulation than the sentence needs.** The totalizing description of Maren’s audience and the convoy’s “one bad afternoon” use breadth or ominousness in place of precision. These are isolated remnants now, not the dominant voice of the drafts. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="44"} :chatgpt-content-reference{index="45"}

# 7. Verdict

**Draft 4 needed.**

1. **Make Piece 3’s opening less dossier-like and its explanation more fluent.** Reduce the accumulation of town-profile details; address the abrupt second-person rules briefing; clarify what is reconstructed and what the scanners actually do; untangle the nested almanac definition. Preserve the causal information needed to understand the confrontation rather than cutting explanation indiscriminately.

2. **Restore the readability of Piece 2’s sensory-building sequence.** Draft 3 makes the reader sort taste and movement simultaneously within a long list. Recover the earlier separation of those processes without restoring all the surrounding exposition.

3. **Remove Piece 2’s remaining overstatement and redundant uncertainty.** The audience’s supposed lifetime non-engagement with art is the conspicuous blanket assertion. The dialogue about the smell’s unknown origin repeats what the narration has just established.

The other flags and regressions do not justify a general polishing round. Piece 1 needs, at most, a local decision about its roster. Piece 4 should be left alone.
