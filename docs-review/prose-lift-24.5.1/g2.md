**Conventions (same as the pilot and the 24.4.x/24.5.0 groups)**
- **Metadata blocks.** Title, Type, Classification and Snapshot are unchanged. The four Outcome lines are unchanged too. Each follows the Full Spectrum pattern (the layer, then a short label), each already says where the person ends up, and none carries a doctrine conflict.
- **Word counts.** A count covers the body plus the Key Lesson (label included), excluding metadata. A word is any whitespace-separated token that contains a letter, digit or `$`. The originals were counted from `simulations.html` the same way.
- **Length cap.** None of the four originals has section headers, so each is capped at 1.0× its original. All four land under it, 0.9–8.3% under, which is short of the guide's 10–25% aim. The originals are dense with named findings and dated numbers and have few aphorisms to cut, and the two dialogue scenes the guide requires cost about what the cuts freed. Keanu Reeves and Mike Tyson are the tightest (0.9% and 1.4% under). Cutting further would mean dropping a ledgered fact or a scene. This could go either way.
- **Ledgers.** One row per doctrinal or factual claim in the original, in the original's order. Claims from the original's lesson line start `KL:`, and row O is the Outcome line.
- **Quote marks in ledgers.** Double-quoted fragments are verbatim from the rewrite, metadata block included (10 words or fewer, italic asterisks ignored). Single-quoted fragments are original wording that was cut. The claim column uses no double quotes.
- **Real people (standing ruling).** All four are real public figures, and none is given a direct quote. What they say is reported ("told her he found it clarifying", "He asked whether the interval was negotiable."). Every line in quotation marks belongs to an invented minor character. No real-world claim is added: the originals keep their professions vague ('creative output', 'extraordinary physical capability', 'historical fraud pattern'), and so do the rewrites, which never name a film, an album, a sport or a firm.
- **No score bands, no ascension applications (standing ruling; DS-32, DS-33, DS-35, DS-36, DS-37).** Applied in all four; see each story's flags. No STI band or floor governs staying in Main, no score justifies a placement, and nobody applies for or is reviewed for ascension. Keanu Reeves phases up when his STI crosses 85. The number is written in because the same v9.0 set names it (the Kurt Cobain card's 'the 85 Sanctuary phasing floor'), so it is the era's own figure. DS-38 and DS-42 (Kurt Cobain) are not in this group.
- **Charter check.** Every VMSS-side statement was read against the current Charter. Art. I lists assault as a single qualifying event for −1, so Mike Tyson's single altercation going to −1 matches the standing ruling (Art. I side of T1). Art. XV makes punitive placement permanent and lets STI shape life inside the layer, which is what the Tyson and Belfort endings say. Art. XIII (signal, not decision) and Art. XII (non-deterministic evaluation) are cited as the current Charter titles them. Two points outside the sweep are flagged rather than resolved: the Threshold Inhibition Protocol in Main (Tyson) and a financial offense going to −2 (Belfort). **Holds: none.**
- **Invented names** (Hesper Kessane, Dell Amaro, Nessa Brisco) were checked against every `.html` and `.md` file in the repo, worktrees excluded, including `docs-review/prose-lift-24.5.1/g1.md`. None is used there. The first-choice names (Tove Anselm, Corin Dahl) were already taken and were replaced.

## 1. Keanu Reeves

**Whose story:** Keanu Reeves. The score that settles at 81, crosses 85 and holds at 94 is his, and so is the one question that tests him in the CCD. The two commuters, the CCD resident and the assessors are around him.

**Rewrite**

```text
Simulation Type: Full Spectrum · Classification: Natural Alignment · Doctrine Snapshot: v9.0
Outcome: +1 Sanctuary — Immediate Ascension Trajectory
```

Keanu Reeves arrived in VMSS at fifty-nine years biologically. His intake evaluation characterized his profile as one of the cleanest behavioral profiles on record for a public figure of his prominence. The STI intake assessment found no manipulation and no coercive conduct patterns. Across decades of documented interaction with colleagues, fans, service workers and strangers, his public behavioral record held no logged instance of deliberate harm to another person. The system had no framework for weighing his unusual profile against any kind of risk model, because there was no risk to model, and it processed the intake as straightforward.

He began in Main Layer, where his STI settled at 81 within the first month, because the system needed time to observe him.

By month four it had observed enough. His interactions with strangers were characteristically direct and genuine, and his conduct in every logged commercial and social exchange was clean. Once, on a crowded transit car, a man drove a cargo case into his shins. "Move, then," the man said. Later a woman by the doors started in on him for blocking them. He made room for the case and let the woman finish. The implant telemetry logged both minor provocations and his response to each: patience without condescension.

In month five his STI crossed 85, and he phased up to +1 Sanctuary.

He qualified for the Cognitive Clarity Domain within two months of arriving in Sanctuary. His quarterly reasoning audits scored high and noted an unusual characteristic: he appeared to have been managing his own cognitive hygiene informally for decades before the CCD formalized the practice. In the margin the assessors wrote:

*Intellectual humility (the genuine willingness to be wrong) more naturally present than in most CCD candidates, who typically acquire it through discipline. In him it appears to be temperament.*

In his seventh month in Sanctuary, after an open compositional session, a CCD resident who had recognized him stopped him at the door.

"Don't you find the certification process reductive?" she asked.

He considered the question for a moment, then told her he found it clarifying: it gave him language for things he had already understood intuitively.

The assessor who later reviewed the implant log of the conversation asked for it to be used as a reference case for explaining the CCD to new applicants.

His STI holds at 94 at last record. His backup vessel revival reliability is rated at the Sanctuary standard, approximately 1 in 1,000,000 probability of revival failure. The system records him as a long-horizon resident with no predicted volatility.

Key lesson: Some people arrive in VMSS already living Sanctuary values. The system's part is to build an environment that finally matches who they are.

## 2. Kanye West

**Whose story:** Kanye West. The eleven-day intake, the 71-to-58 drop and the climb back to 69 all land on his own ledger. The assessor, his assistant and the counselor are invented minor characters around him.

**Rewrite**

```text
Kanye West
Simulation Type: Full Spectrum · Classification: Contested Equilibrium · Doctrine Snapshot: v9.0
Outcome: Main Layer (0) — Sustained but Volatile
```

Kanye West's intake evaluation took eleven days, the longest conducted in the simulation's recorded case history. His profile generated internal contradictions, and each one required explicit resolution before placement. His contribution metrics were extraordinary: creative output, demonstrated cultural influence, technical innovation in multiple domains. They stood in direct tension with a trust breach record that was equally extensive.

On the eleventh day an assessor named Hesper Kessane read him the placement: Main Layer, at an STI of 64. He asked her what his work had counted for.

"It's in the file," she said. "This number reads behavioral reliability."

The system did not grade on cultural significance, and it considered 64 accurate. In the placement file the Meritboard noted that he sat at the intersection of the civilization's two foundational tensions: extraordinary positive contribution, and documented patterns of harm to individuals through public humiliation, coercive social pressure and trust violation.

His first year was productive in ways the system recorded without comment. His creative output in VMSS was significant. Together, neural diving infrastructure and the absence of the market pressures that had historically distorted his Earth work produced pieces the cultural record will note. His STI climbed to 71 by month eight.

In month fourteen he contested a logged trust breach involving a collaborator. The public STI ledger dispute escalated through the implant-linked legal interpretation system and was resolved against him. The ruling was accurate and documented.

For the next 72 hours his implant logged a sustained intent pattern. The enforcement network monitored it without acting, because no harm threshold was crossed. The STI system recorded it as a major escalation indicator, and his score dropped from 71 to 58 in a single logged event.

On the fourth morning his assistant read the new number off her handset. "Fifty-eight," she said. He had her reopen the piece he was working on.

By month twenty his STI had recovered to 69. The recovery trajectory was genuine. He had completed voluntary rehabilitation under Article XV trajectory evaluation, and the implant telemetry confirmed the change was not performative. On the last day of the rehabilitation he asked his counselor whether 58 could have cost him Main.

"Not on its own," she said. "Article Thirteen. The score's a signal."

He is in Main Layer at last record. The record does not say whether he ever reaches 85.

Key lesson: In the VMSS ledger, extraordinary contribution and harm sit side by side, and neither offsets the other. Main Layer holds both until the pattern resolves.

## 3. Mike Tyson

**Whose story:** Mike Tyson. The protocol stops his own arm, the recalibration reaches him through a friend's word, and the −1 reassignment lands on him. The man in the food hall, Dell Amaro and the man in the venue are invented minor characters.

**Rewrite**

```text
Mike Tyson
Simulation Type: Full Spectrum · Classification: Controlled Descent · Doctrine Snapshot: v9.0
Outcome: -1 Noncompliance — Post-Intervention Working as Designed
```

Mike Tyson's intake evaluation classified his profile as high-variance: extraordinary physical capability, a documented violent conduct history, genuine periods of transformation and recalibration, and a psychological architecture the behavioral assessment tools described as deeply reactive under specific triggering conditions. His STI intake score was 52. The multi-factor evaluation placed him in Main Layer. It evaluated present trajectory, and on historical conduct alone his risk model did not meet the threshold for punitive placement.

He opted into the Threshold Inhibition Protocol at intake, and the implant did significant work from day one. In his first month the TIP engaged twice, both times on physical confrontation triggers in crowded social environments, and both times it caught the escalation before the harm threshold.

The second time was in a food hall at the dinner rush. A man shouldered into him. "Watch yourself," the man said. Tyson's right arm came up, stopped halfway, and stayed there until the man had walked off. No reassignment followed. He was aware of both interventions, and after the second he took a voluntary 36-hour withdrawal from public spaces. The behavioral telemetry read it as genuine processing rather than strategic compliance management.

Dell Amaro, who trained at the same gym and whose opinion Tyson respected, told him about the voluntary neural recalibration program.

"It helped me," Dell said. "Slow, though."

He enrolled on his own. By month six his STI had improved to 61. The implant was still logging intent escalations, because the triggering architecture was present and did not disappear, but the escalation-to-threshold rate was declining. The program was working on him in the specific way it works for people who arrive with genuine motivation: slowly and non-linearly, but measurably.

In month nine, in a public venue, a man found the provocation pattern the implant had flagged as high-risk in his behavioral model.

"Go on," the man said, close to his face. "Go on, then."

The TIP engaged only partially and slowed the act without stopping it entirely. The altercation crossed the harm threshold before the protocol could complete its intervention. The harm was logged, and his reassignment to -1 was immediate. He did not contest it. The system noted his acceptance as understanding, consistent with his recalibration trajectory.

In -1, the Balanced Layer, his STI stabilized at 49. The recalibration program continued, and though the triggering architecture was still present in the telemetry, the escalation rate kept declining. Placement in -1 is permanent. Within it, his STI trajectory determines his standing: access to better districts, better contracts, and a coaching role the system recognizes as qualifying work.

At last record he coaches with a youth physical development program, 20-hour qualifying weeks with the primary job subsidy active, and spends the remainder of his time training independently.

Key lesson: Post-intervention answers a completed act immediately, accurately and in proportion, and -1 stops escalation short of the most severe threshold. A disciplined, goal-oriented person can build a life there that fits.
