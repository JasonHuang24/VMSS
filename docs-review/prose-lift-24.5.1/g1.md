## 1. Jeffrey Dahmer

**Whose story:** Jeffrey Dahmer, seen from outside. The implant watches him from his first days, the warning and the reassignment land on him, and the one completed act lands on an unnamed victim who is revived.

**Rewrite**

```text
Jeffrey Dahmer
Simulation Type: Criminal Profile · Classification: Capital Harm · Doctrine Snapshot: pre-v5.0
Outcome: Unconstrained — Rapid Reassignment to -3 Terminal
```

Jeffrey Dahmer kept a low profile for his first several days in Main Layer. "Found the market yet?" Anders Lusk from the next unit asked him on the stairs, and Dahmer said he had and thanked him.

He was observant and patient, and the system recorded both without alarm, since cognition alone carried no threshold weight. His implant was logging the pattern of his neural activity, as it logged every resident's. From early in his residency it detected persistent predatory intent signals. Its warning thresholds were calibrated to the transition from intent to execution, which he had not yet made, and it issued no warning.

Within the first week he began making contact with individuals through the neural network. The overtures were ostensibly social and structurally consistent with grooming patterns. His implant's intent monitoring detected the escalation and issued a formal warning. He disabled the failsafe.

The first act of capital harm was a murder. It triggered an immediate frame-skip, and his reassignment to -3 Terminal was executed within seconds of the act's completion.

The victim was revived in Main Layer's medical facility.

"Where is he now?"

"-3 Terminal," the attending said. "There's no revival down there."

In -3 he continued the behavior patterns that had produced his reassignment, until the terminal layer's self-regulating dynamics removed him permanently.

Key lesson: Reassignment waits for an executed act, so one capital harm completes first. VMSS accepts that cost over punishing intent alone.

## 2. Osama Bin Laden

**Whose story:** Osama Bin Laden, seen from outside. Each threshold reaches him as an implant warning, the rally is where speech turns into planning, and −3 is where his authority stops working.

**Rewrite**

```text
Osama Bin Laden
Simulation Type: Criminal Profile · Classification: Ideological Violence · Doctrine Snapshot: pre-v5.0
Outcome: Unconstrained — Rapid Reassignment to -3 Terminal
```

Osama Bin Laden entered Main Layer and began operating within the protected speech framework immediately. VMSS protects speech, religious and political included, until it crosses into coordinated incitement to harm. His early forum posts were ideologically charged and, initially, stayed within the threshold.

"Won't the implant flag this?" a reader asked.

He said it wouldn't.

The escalation was deliberate and structured. Over ten days his public communications shifted from theological framing toward explicit calls for violence against specific groups. He took the implant's warnings as confirmation that the system was what he had called it, an instrument of the forces he defined himself against. Each warning sped him up.

At a neural rally a man asked, "When do we start?" The rally became a coordinated planning session for organized harm. The system detected the transition from protected speech to actionable incitement: intent plus organized execution planning had crossed the criminal escalation threshold. Reassignment to -3 Terminal followed within days.

In -3 he tried to establish ideological authority within the layer's territorial structure. The environment did not respond to it. Within months he was killed in factional violence. No revival occurred.

Key lesson: VMSS protects speech substantively and precisely, up to the organization of harm. The limit is mechanical, whatever the politics.

## 3. Thomas Reed

**Whose story:** Thomas Reed. The flag meets him at the checkpoint, every door he knocks on answers it, and the four-year appeal is decided on him.

**Rewrite**

```text
Thomas Reed
Simulation Type: Criminal Profile · Classification: Punitive Reassignment / Attempted Reformation · Doctrine Snapshot: v17.9.4
Outcome: Functional but limited life in -3 — permanent social isolation despite reformed conduct
```

Thomas Reed dropped into -3 Terminal with a permanent flag for child sexual exploitation. Every cooperative, reputation network and private security outfit saw it the second he cleared the boundary checkpoint.

No one immediately dragged him into a cell; -3 didn't have the institutional bandwidth to jail every monster on arrival. The layer's organic order read the file instead. The foreman at the first cooperative he asked for work took one look at his record.

"Fuck off," he said.

At the second cooperative they had already heard. Within two weeks Reed was doing the lowest, dirtiest manual labor nobody else wanted and living in the most marginal corner of the district.

He actually tried. He kept his head down, crossed the lane whenever children came down it, took whatever qualifying work was offered, and never once contested the flag. He even completed the voluntary behavioral recalibration programs the layer had. By all measurable standards he was reformed. He kept the certificates in a tin under his cot.

The flag never cleared, and the locals never forgot. He built a small, isolated life: basic shelter, enough tokens to eat, the quiet autonomy the frontier grudgingly allowed. People tolerated him the way they would a venomous snake that kept to its corner. He was never hunted or tortured.

After four years he applied for a minor reputation adjustment through the local cooperative's private justice channel. He brought the tin. The arbiter went through the certificates one at a time and handed them back.

"Denied," she said.

The ledger entry stayed. Reed shrugged, put the certificates back in the tin, and went back to work.

Key lesson: -3 adds no suffering of its own for punitive residents, and their records remain public. Where reputation is the only real currency, a permanent flag is a social fact for life.
