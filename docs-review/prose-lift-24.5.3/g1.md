## 1. Kanye West

**Whose story:** Kanye West. The eleven-day intake, the review that opens at month ten and is suspended after the 71-to-58 drop, and the climb back to 69, a point under the floor, all land on his own ledger. The assessor, his assistant and the counselor are invented minor characters around him.

**Rewrite**

```text
Kanye West
Simulation Type: Full Spectrum · Classification: Contested Equilibrium · Doctrine Snapshot: v9.0 · Off-canon
Outcome: Main Layer (0) — Sustained but Volatile
```

Kanye West's intake evaluation took eleven days, the longest conducted in the simulation's recorded case history. His profile generated internal contradictions, and each one required explicit resolution before placement. His contribution metrics were extraordinary: creative output, demonstrated cultural influence, technical innovation in multiple domains. They stood in direct tension with a trust breach record that was equally extensive.

On the eleventh day an assessor named Hesper Kessane read him the placement: Main Layer, at an STI of 64. He asked her what his work had counted for.

"It's in the file," she said. "This number reads behavioral reliability."

The system did not grade on cultural significance, and it considered 64 accurate. In the placement file the Meritboard noted that he sat at the intersection of the civilization's two foundational tensions: extraordinary positive contribution, and documented patterns of harm to individuals through public humiliation, coercive social pressure and trust violation.

His first year was productive in ways the system recorded without comment. His creative output in VMSS was significant. Together, neural diving infrastructure and the absence of the market pressures that had historically distorted his Earth work produced pieces the cultural record will note. His STI climbed to 71 by month eight, and he qualified for residency renewal without issue. At month ten the Meritboard's ascension eligibility review opened provisionally.

In month fourteen he contested a logged trust breach involving a collaborator. The public STI ledger dispute escalated through the implant-linked legal interpretation system and was resolved against him. The ruling was accurate and documented.

For the next 72 hours his implant logged a sustained intent pattern. The enforcement network monitored it without acting, because no harm threshold was crossed. The STI system recorded it as a major escalation indicator, and his score dropped from 71 to 58 in a single logged event.

On the fourth morning his assistant read the new number off her handset. "Fifty-eight," she said. "Review's suspended." He had her reopen the piece he was working on.

By month twenty his STI had recovered to 69. The recovery trajectory was genuine. He had completed voluntary rehabilitation under Article XV trajectory evaluation, and the implant telemetry confirmed the change was not performative. On the last day of the rehabilitation he asked his counselor whether 58 could have cost him Main.

"Not on its own," she said. "Article Thirteen. The score's a signal."

He is in Main Layer at last record, one point below the Main band's 70 floor, the score still in -1 territory. The Meritboard notes the proximity. The record does not say whether he gets there.

Key lesson: In the VMSS ledger, extraordinary contribution and harm sit side by side, and neither offsets the other. Main Layer holds both until the pattern resolves.

## 2. Jordan Belfort

**Whose story:** Jordan Belfort. He tries to negotiate the monitoring on his first day, files the application the monitoring log sinks, contests the decision, and builds the scheme the ledger system catches. The intake officer and Nessa Brisco are invented minor characters.

**Rewrite**

```text
Jordan Belfort
Simulation Type: Full Spectrum · Classification: Systematic Breach · Doctrine Snapshot: v9.0 · Off-canon
Outcome: -2 Violent Offense — Pattern Reassignment
```

Jordan Belfort's intake evaluation placed him in Main Layer at an STI of 58. The behavioral assessment noted high intelligence, high initiative, and a documented pattern of treating institutional boundaries as negotiating positions rather than fixed constraints. The system recorded this as relevant. On Earth conduct alone, the multi-factor evaluation found, his historical fraud pattern did not meet the threshold for punitive placement. It flagged his profile as high-manipulation-risk and assigned elevated monitoring parameters.

"Your implant telemetry will be reviewed at a shorter interval than standard," the intake officer told him.

He asked whether the interval was negotiable.

"No," she said.

By surface metrics, his first year in Main Layer was successful. He identified economic opportunities quickly, built relationships efficiently, and accumulated resources at a rate the system noted as consistent with his capability profile. His STI climbed to 68 by month seven. The ascension eligibility review opened at month nine, and he filed the application.

The system, reviewing his elevated monitoring log, declined the application. It documented eight instances of borderline manipulation in his recorded social and commercial interactions. None of them individually crossed a threshold. In aggregate, the multi-factor evaluation classified them as a pattern of systematic trust exploitation. He contested the decision through the legal interpretation system. The contest was evaluated and dismissed, and the pattern classification stood.

Over the following six months he escalated. His implant telemetry began logging intent patterns that moved from manipulation-adjacent to coercive. He had studied the multi-factor evaluation model carefully, and the financial scheme he built was a sophisticated attempt to game it. It was structured to extract value from three other Main Layer residents through false representation and deliberate information asymmetry, and designed to operate below every individual threshold while crossing them in aggregate.

At month eighteen he took the third of them, Nessa Brisco, through the terms.

"The other two are already in?" she asked.

He told her they were.

The ledger system detected and logged the scheme before its completion, and the record set its pieces side by side, each one below its own threshold. The system classified the whole as a qualifying coercive financial offense. The harm was documented, and his reassignment to -2 was immediate and permanent.

He is in -2 at last record. The intelligence and initiative that drove his entire behavioral history are still present. What they produce in -2 is not yet determined.

Key lesson: Article XII's non-deterministic evaluation principle exists specifically because single-metric gaming is predictable. Pattern recognition across multiple signals, time and context is not so easily circumvented.
