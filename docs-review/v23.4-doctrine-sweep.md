# v23.4.0 — Full-Canon Doctrine Sweep: Disposition Record

**Date:** 2026-07-24. **Baseline:** main `04ec450` (v23.3.0, tag → `5866a4c`). **Scope:** consistency-and-integrity pass over every canon surface — root `*.html`, `documents/`, `docs-review/` — for cross-file contradictions, number/date drift, citation drift, R13 layer-doctrine leaks, R16 house-style violations, and hedge erosion. Process-tier document; the canon-facing texts are the files it names.

**Not** a pressure test and **not** a doctrine-invention pass. No new doctrine, no new laws, no new Charter text, no glossary additions. Register unchanged at 96 (8 Charter / 65 Federal / 23 Regulatory), LP-001–LP-082. Glossary unchanged at 125.

## Method

Thirteen lens-specific readers over the corpus (numbers, eras, tax, citations, R13, R16, hedges, Charter-vs-whitepaper, entry surfaces, layers, register-vs-Code, Path 2, version stamps), producing 66 findings. Every citation was then validated mechanically — quote must appear verbatim at the cited line — and **66/66 verified exactly, zero fabricated citations.** Each finding then faced two independent skeptics prompted to refute, on separate lenses: one on fact and build mechanics (re-derive the claim, resolve the counter-surface, check whether the edit trips `check-canon`, check whether the target is generated output), one on doctrine steelman (deliberate hedge? in-world narrative? closed-queue item? "gap"? would the edit smuggle in doctrine?). Both defaulted to *refuted* under uncertainty.

Outcome: **45 refuted, 13 flagged, 8 cleared unanimously.** Four of the flagged/split findings were promoted to fixes on adjudication (recorded below with the reason). A separate deterministic pass over every internal link, anchor, duplicate id, `Whitepaper §N` reference and `Article <roman>` reference found **zero** defects.

The adversarial pass earned its place: it overturned several findings that had survived direct verification, on convention knowledge a first read does not surface. Those are recorded in §3 rather than discarded, so a later sweep does not re-flag them.

## 2. Flagged — real, but not the sweep's call

Each of these is a verified inconsistency whose *repair* requires an authorial decision. None was edited.

1. **`whitepaper.html:1097` — "structurally impossible" vs "improbable" (hedge divergence).** §18.2 says the layered observation architecture makes wrongful factual determination "structurally impossible"; `charter.html:302` and `whitepaper.html:1184` both say "improbable". **Already docketed:** `docs-review/vmss-laws-latent-mining/mine-wp-17-19.md:233` and `:530` record this exact divergence — "Hedge divergence flagged verbatim; not resolved here" — and that pass sits at the Phase M gate awaiting veto. Fixing it here would pre-empt a pending decision. Two further reasons for caution: "structurally impossible" is a 16-occurrence corpus idiom whose contrast is *architectural vs policy-prohibited* (`documents/resources-source.html:529`), and `whitepaper.html:584` deliberately holds both notions in one sentence. Also note Article XV's remedial class (corrupted ledger, identity-system failure, AI processing error) is a *different* failure class from determination on an intact record, so "impossible" does not empty the remedy.
2. **`layer--3.html:168` — voluntary permanent resident "retains full upward mobility".** Contradicts the Ceiling Seal as canon now stands (`faq.html:635`, `why-vmss.html:532`, glossary). **But the section is stamped "Doctrine Snapshot: v9.0" (`layer--3.html:144`), the sentence entered at v9.0 (`18f771d`), and at that snapshot the Charter said the opposite** ("Voluntary descent does not permanently close the upward pathway"). The Ceiling Seal and the elective/permanent split arrived later (`38b0daa`, v17.9.9). Era-faithful to its own stamp; refreshing it is an authorial choice, and the scope would be `layer--3.html:168` plus the `simulations.html` mirror of the same profile.
3. **`law-polling.html:2548` — LP-078 cites Whitepaper §21.5, which does not exist** (chapter 21 ends at §21.4). The dead citation is certain; the target is not. LP-078 is wholly an MGD statute, so re-pointing it at §20.4/§20.6 would move it into the *SAD* chapter and blur a distinction the whitepaper spends a section maintaining ("21.1 How MGDs Differ from SADs"). Safer candidates: chapter-level "§21 (Metric Gated Domains)", or naming the instrument instead of a section.
4. **`law-polling.html:768` and `:1034` — two Canon Anchors cite World §19 for material it does not contain.** `world.html:19` is Territorial Doctrine. ## 3. Refuted — examined, confirmed *not* defects

Recorded so a later sweep does not re-flag them. The strongest cases:

- **`founder-calibratable` is a deliberate registered knob, not an R13 leak.** Four findings proposed deleting it from `whitepaper.html:924-925` and `law-polling.html:2310`, `:2340`. It is an intentional marker: `docs-review/v21.9-FLAGS.md:88-90` lists "Founder knobs left open" including the attribution-vulnerable settlement schedule, and `docs-review/flags.md:25` records the 12-month commercial-idleness window as "marked founder-calibratable in all three engravings, per 1c". Deleting the marker would silently convert an open parameter into a settled one.
- **Commit hashes in the register are a labelled convention.** `Provenance | Excavated · commit XXXX · site vN.N` appears on the rate-chain entries (`law-polling.html:2378`, `:2398`, `:2421`) with `rate-history.html`'s era table built on it — the register openly recording that these historical strata were reconstructed from the repo's own history. Removing one instance (`:2418`) would break the idiom and leave three standing. Relatedly, `law-polling.html:191` is a **code comment**, not rendered text.
- **A Canon Anchor's parenthetical names the receiving instrument, not the whitepaper heading.** Proposed "fix" of `law-polling.html:2580` ("§12.4 (Central Banking Authority)" → §12.3) is wrong: the *same line* cites "§17.2 (Continuity Integrity)" where §17.2 is titled "Continuity Not Innocence", and *The Continuity Integrity Act* is a named Code instrument — as is *The Central Banking Authority*. The idiom recurs at `:2422`, `:2446`, `:2483`. §12.4 (Asset Treatment on Layer Movement) is the correct pointer for LP-079's currency-wall clause.
- **Category blurbs are exemplar lists, not registers.** `simulations-resources.html:47` names 9 of 15 VMSS Advanced resources; the sibling hub card names only 7 of all 33, and the author's own commit language calls them "category exemplars". No exhaustiveness contract.
- **Version stamps at 23.3.0 were not a defect at sweep time**, and the proposal to bump one file alone would have failed CI. Handled as a paired release action instead (§1).
- **Both dangling Article designations remain legitimate**, as already recorded at `docs-review/vmss-laws-codification-proposal.md:305`: Article XXIX is LP-001, a *failed* amendment (correct as a frozen record), and Article XXXIV is far-future speculative narrative.
- **`documents/resources-source.html:1465`** — "two years" is correct under an elapsed-time reading, and neither correction is uniquely determined by the sentence.
- **`technologies.html:75`** ("Completely opt-in and removable") reads against `:56` (mandatory in Sanctuary) but is card-scoped privacy framing; a rewrite is voice, not repair.
- **Closed queues stayed closed.** Nothing in this sweep reopens the v21 or v23 pressure-test programs, and no finding was allowed to surface as a queue item. The five named no-action rulings in `docs-review/v23-queue-closure.md` were used as refutation grounds, not revisited.
