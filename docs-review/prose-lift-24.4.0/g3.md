## 1. The Saurian Park

**Whose story:** Lorant Veres, the operator. The refusal, the price of descent, the colosseum ruling and the visitors' fares all land on him, and he is the one counting heads at the gate.

**Rewrite**

```text
The Saurian Park
Simulation Type: Civilizational Stress Test · Classification: Voluntary Permanent Residency, Lifetime Investment, Terminal Layer Operation · Doctrine Snapshot: v16.5
Outcome: Permitted in -3, Endorsed by Local Sentiment, Operator Reassigned by Choice
```

Lorant Veres had built his reputation on the cold financial discipline that took him from a single construction crew to a continental operation. As a Main Layer industrialist he held majority ownership of one of the larger construction firms in the upper layers and of a bio-engineering laboratory whose work on extinct-species genetic reconstruction had gone far enough that the species were no longer extinct in any meaningful sense. For a decade the laboratory's results had been published, audited and quietly admired. Veres had never published what he wanted them for. He wanted to build the park from the films of two centuries earlier, the one every child who grew up on them had imagined: dinosaurs roaming free, and dangerous.

He filed for Council certification, and the rejection came back within six weeks. It objected to nothing in principle. It quoted his own technical specifications back to him: the reconstructed predators were apex carnivores whose feeding behaviors could not be scripted and whose hunger states could not be capped, and the actuarial models estimated guest fatalities at one death per several thousand visitors. In the upper layers, under the harm provisions, each death would generate a harm record against him and trigger TIP-adjacent review under the standing animal-attack provisions, and the reassignment liability would accumulate past anything waiver language could absorb. Attached was a compromise. The predators could stay if their jaws were physically constrained and their feeding states pharmacologically managed, if the dangerous sections were observed only from armoured vehicles, and if the inhabitant-prey relationship was severed at the substrate level. Veres set the document down on his desk and did not respond for three days.

"It's a zoo," he told his counsel on the third day.

"It's the best zoo on the planet."

"The predators are in harnesses and the guests are behind glass. It's a zoo."

"Lorant, they are never going to let your raptors eat your customers up here, however much the customers want it," his counsel said.

The other option was -3. He spent the first week dismissing it as the kind of thing only desperate men consider. In the second week he sketched the logistics on paper in his private study and found them difficult, and possible. The construction expertise was in his own firm and the bio-engineering in his own laboratory. The talent he would need was in the upper layers and could be persuaded with enough compensation. Below, -3's institutional withdrawal meant the harm provisions that blocked him here did not apply. In the third week he ran the numbers on what the park would cost. In the fourth he ran them on what it would cost him.

The figure at the bottom of that page was the whole fortune. He would descend voluntarily under Article III.V, and his Main Layer holdings would convert to Terminal Layer tokens through the progressive retention schedule, which was federal law: 10% retained on the first $1M, scaling down to 1% above $1B. Below the figure he listed the rest: his Main Layer life and standing, his friends, the ascension trajectory his STI had been quietly building for two decades, his implant configuration, his body. The Ceiling Seal would make the descent permanent. There was no provisional version and no two-year trial.

He deliberated for several more weeks. His advisors expected him to call the park a fantasy and go back to expanding the laboratory's commercial portfolio. His family talked around it at dinner and waited for him to let the dream die in his study. His legal counsel prepared the papers for a quiet abandonment of the certification request, with the laboratory's relevant research divisions reassigned to less doctrinally explosive applications. Everything else Veres had wanted to build, he had built, most of it after someone told him it could not be done. On a morning in early spring he decided the price was acceptable.

He filed for voluntary permanent residency under Article III.V, and the Council acknowledged the filing quietly, neither celebrating it nor obstructing it. The retention schedule, punitive by design, left him a Terminal Layer fortune that was vast by -3 standards and a real, irreversible loss against what he had given up. He arrived in -3 with the implant configuration it issued its residents and nothing above it, with no Main Layer recourse, no upper-layer legal standing and no path back. The Ceiling Seal closed behind him on the day he arrived. He did not look at it. He had been looking at the construction site coordinates for a month.

The construction took years. He recruited the talent one person at a time. Some came for compensation that only made sense to people whose own situations had already brought them to -3 or to its boundary. Others were among the few engineers and biologists for whom the chance to build something that had never been built was compensation enough. His upper-layer firm no longer belonged to him, but its techniques came down with the people he convinced to descend with him. The laboratory's research had gone ahead of him, transferred under his Article III.V provisions to a -3 facility he had funded before his own descent. The predators came online in batches, into enclosures built for authenticity over containment in ways the upper layers would never have permitted. By then his new firm operated inside the park's own boundaries, and it walled the perimeter against the surrounding -3 districts. The fatalities began with the construction crews, as he had known they would. They went into the operating cost.

The park opened to -3 visitors first. Veres expected the indifference the layer usually showed upper-layer enterprises that came down into its territory. On the third afternoon of the first guest tour, a velociraptor pack pursued a visitor and killed him. The news spread through the surrounding districts within hours, and the commentary kept using one word: colosseum.

-3 had colosseums. They were a recognized category of place in the layer's customary law, older than Veres's project and older than the Charter provision that had let him descend, and each was defined by the contract its gate enforced. A visitor paid to go through at their own risk, with death on the table at even odds with survival, and knew it before the gate closed behind them. The risk declaration on Veres's gate, which hid nothing about what waited inside the perimeter, had been there since opening day.

"You'll hear it all week," said Oda Stroud, who ran his perimeter crew. "He knew what was in there when he walked in."

"And me?"

"Nobody here calls that an injury." She was already walking back to the gate.

No inquest followed. -3's private justice infrastructure left colosseum deaths alone, as it left alone a duel both parties had agreed to, and no jurisdiction in the layer had standing to prosecute him for the deaths or to shut him down. Nobody detained Veres, and no review board or cooperative looked into his conduct.

Reviewers from the voluntary community in -3's better districts came to examine the park's operational parameters. They walked the perimeter, read the risk declaration posted at the gate, and confirmed that the colosseum classification had attached, permanently, on the day the first paying visitor walked through. Veres had never applied for it; -3 issued no licences. The punitive districts, whose private justice infrastructure handled most of -3's enforcement, reached the same conclusion through their own channels and filed it the same way.

Upper-layer visitors began arriving within the first year. They came as visitors, and the visitor protocols held: vessel link active, federal surveillance floor recording, return path intact. Veres charged them a multiple of the resident price. They paid in Terminal Layer tokens bought with upper-layer currency through the authorized downward channel, at the central bank's settlement rate. Their fatalities were rare, because visitor vessels carried protective configurations the resident bodies did not, but not zero. Each one was logged at the federal floor and filed to the archive that held the Severance Show records and the Frontier Park behavioral data. The Council watched and did not intervene.

An upper-layer journalist asked him once whether he regretted the descent. "It was the entry fee," Veres said. The evening tour was coming back through the gate, and he went on counting heads.

Civilizational Note: The upper layers won't certify a park that predictably kills its guests. An operator who wants one has to move to -3 for good, and -3 decides whether it lasts.

A student asked Nasrin once what Year One had been like. She told him about the woman on the fourth floor.
