Emil Straka was inside a jeweler's on Tessel Street, with a pistol halfway out of his coat, when the failsafe warnings started. His implant had read the violent intent and the motor preparation within milliseconds, and the motor inhibition safeguard locked his arm at the elbow. He overrode it and raised the gun to the clerk's face.

"Open the cases," he said.

She was the only staff behind the counter. She put both hands flat on the glass and looked past him at the door.

The shop's environmental sensors, the AR context system and his own implant telemetry all confirmed an armed threat in progress, and the system had crossed its enforcement threshold. The autonomous enforcement network's drones came in low through the open door. The sedative took him before he could turn. He woke restrained, in transport to judicial intake.

The reviewer played the incident record in three panes: his implant telemetry, the shop's sensor data and an AR reconstruction of the counter. He watched his own arm stop at the elbow and then keep rising.

"I want to appeal," he said.

"Not the record." The reviewer closed the panes.

After the review he was reassigned to -2 Violent Offense. His STI ledger took a permanent public entry: *violent coercion*.

Key lesson: Armed coercion goes straight to the criminal pathway, and the implant record is the evidence.

## 2. Relational Trust Breach

**Whose story:** Nadia Ferro. The score, the partnership and the endorsements are all hers, and every consequence reaches her as an entry, a filing or a call.

**Rewrite**

```text
Relational Trust Breach
Simulation Type: STI Scenario · Classification: Non-Criminal Breach · Doctrine Snapshot: v8.0
Outcome: STI Impact — Social Consequence Without Criminal Enforcement
```

Nadia Ferro and Tomas Hale had registered their partnership as exclusive, and the agreement spelled out its terms. In March, on a work trip, she slept with a man from the client's team, outside those terms. Her implant logged it, and so did the hotel's AR context system.

Infidelity wasn't a crime, and no drone came. She lay awake until the window went gray.

Her STI had read 90 the evening before. In the morning it read 62, with one line beneath it: *Major relational trust breach. Public ledger.*

Tomas was at the kitchen table when she got home, the entry open on his handset.

"When did you see it?" she said.

"At six." He turned the handset face down. "I filed for dissolution at nine."

Her professional network read the entry too. Several of them had endorsements sitting on her ledger page, next to the new line. Ilse Brenner called the next afternoon.

"I'm pulling my endorsement," Ilse said. "I wanted you to hear it from me."

Within forty-eight hours two endorsements were gone: Ilse's, and one from an engineer Nadia had worked with once, who didn't call.

No reassignment notice came. What did come, over the summer, came slowly: a trust-gated contract that stopped opening to her, and clients who took their work elsewhere.

In October she read the entry again, late at night: the date, the classification, 90 and 62. There was no line about whether she deserved forgiveness. The entry was permanent. She closed the page and turned off the lamp.

Key lesson: The STI reaches harms that break no law. It makes the conduct visible and leaves the response to the people who can see it.

## 3. The Threshold

**Whose story:** Lise Arden. Her brother's speeding record reaches her across his dinner table, and her own evening then goes the other way. The two residents become siblings, so both outcomes land on one person.

**Rewrite**

```text

Simulation Type: STI Scenario · Classification: Layer Assignment Threshold · Doctrine Snapshot: v10.6

```

On Thursday Lise Arden had dinner at her brother's flat, two streets from her own in the same Main Layer district. Ruben had been caught speeding that week, and he slid the notice across the table: 94 km/h in a 60 km/h zone. His implant had flagged it automatically, from the environmental sensors' velocity data, corroborated by his own positional telemetry. Below that sat a minor STI adjustment on the private ledger tier, under the threshold for public visibility, and a fine in Baseline Credit already charged to his account. There was no judicial review. The notice ended: *Clearable through remediation. Multi-factor evaluation, each infraction and the pattern: no change to layer.*

"That's eleven in four years," Lise said. "You drive too fast."

"Paid every one." Ruben took the notice back. "Score's been climbing six months straight. And there was nobody on the road."

"There might have been."

He filled her glass again.

Lise's implant logged a blood-alcohol equivalent at the table, read from her physiological markers, and a second reading forty minutes later confirmed it. Ruben offered her the couch. She said she was fine to drive.

At the curb, before she opened the car door, the implant warned her and offered the failsafe. She dismissed the warning and got in. The car moved. The road was quiet, and she passed nobody on the way home.

The reassignment order reached her before midnight. It gave her evening back to her in four lines: the two readings, the warning, the dismissal, the car moving. Under them it said: *Single qualifying event. Following judicial review: reassignment to -1 Noncompliance, immediate. STI ledger: public entry.*

She read it twice, looking for a line that was wrong. Then she called Ruben and read it to him.

"What's the fine?" he said.

"There isn't one."

"You've never even had a speeding ticket."

"I know."

She woke the next morning in -1 Noncompliance.

Key lesson: One severe act, such as driving impaired, can move someone from Main Layer to -1 on its own. Minor infractions get there only as a sustained pattern that is never corrected.

## 4. The Coalition Breach Attempt

**Whose story:** Ivo Kessler, the original's observer half a kilometer back. He is made a -3 crew leader who turned the coordinator down twice. The pitch, the night and the after-action record all reach him, and the plume's sorting lands on him.

**Rewrite**

```text
The Coalition Breach Attempt
Simulation Type: Civilizational Stress Test · Classification: Organized Sovereign Threat · Doctrine Snapshot: v10.4.1
Outcome: National Defense Track — Full Military Response
```

Lucan Marsh came to Ivo Kessler's yard in the first year, when he was still only talking. For months he had been walking between four districts of -3 Terminal, introducing crews to one another, and he carried a paper map with each crew's territory inked on it, the boundaries that had kept them apart.

"Every breach that's failed was one crew going at the wall alone," Marsh said.

"How many crews have you got?"

"None yet." He folded the map. "Ask me next year."

By the second year Marsh had agreements. Ivo had never known two -3 crews to share a well, and by the third year Marsh's crews were drilling as one fighting force on the flats outside Ivo's district.

Marsh came back three weeks before the operation, three years after he had started, and laid a rifle across Ivo's workbench.

"It came over the boundary in pieces," he said. "Eighteen months of that, off the black market. We're about two hundred now. Fourteen crews. You'd make fifteen."

"And the kill switch? It's in their own doctrine."

"Sixty percent of us have had our implants cut out these last weeks." Marsh nodded at a crate his men had set down by the gate. It was unmarked, and he didn't say where it had come from. "Dampeners for the rest."

He unfolded the map again. He had studied the wall's sensor integration, and two junctions were circled in red where he believed the coverage had gaps. Under them he had written a date, a night when the patrols ran thin. Ivo said no again.

Ivo watched from a slag heap half a kilometer back. At 0340 the coalition moved out in three columns toward the junctions, and then the columns stopped. The kill switch had fired on every registered identifier in the operational zone, and eighty people dropped at the same instant. Four seconds later the nanobot plume reached the other hundred and twenty, the ones who had cut their implants out to escape the switch.

Two hundred people fell, and Ivo heard nothing. There was no explosion, and he could not see what had reached them. He counted the seconds under his breath from the first man falling, and it was over before ninety. No aircraft came over, and no troops crossed into -3. The wall was never touched.
