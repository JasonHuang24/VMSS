**Conventions (same as the pilot and the 24.4.x/24.5.x groups)**
- **Metadata blocks.** The frozen fields on these cards are the title, the Cyberpunk 2077 Element and VMSS Analog fields (the collection's Type and Classification) and the Snapshot. All four are unchanged. All four Outcome lines are unchanged too: they are the collection's label style and already name what happens. Two of them end on adjective triplets ('Boring, Functional, Real'; 'Chosen, Functional, Free'). Plain rewrites are possible if Jason wants the collection's seven labels changed together; this could go either way.
- **Lesson box.** All four cards label it "Civilizational Note". The label is kept and the box is held to the Key Lesson rules, as the 24.4.0 groups did.
- **Word counts.** A count covers the body plus the Civilizational Note (label included), excluding metadata. A word is any whitespace-separated token that contains a letter, digit or `$`. Originals were counted straight from `simulations.html` the same way.
- **Length cap.** The originals are mapping essays, not headed case studies, so each rewrite is capped at 1.0× the original, as 24.5.4 g1 did. All four come in under it. This could go either way: an essay turned into a story is close to the 1.5× case-study rule, but none of the four needed the room.
- **Ledgers.** One row per doctrinal or factual claim in the original, in the original's order. Rows prefixed CN are claims from the original's Civilizational Note, and row O is the Outcome line.
- **Quote marks in ledgers.** Double-quoted fragments are verbatim from the rewrite (10 words or fewer, metadata block included, italic asterisks ignored). Single-quoted fragments are original wording that was cut or changed. The claim column uses no double quotes.
- **Night City material.** Each original runs two sides: a Night City description and its VMSS analog. The stories are set in VMSS. Where the Night City side carries facts the story needs, they arrive through an old game a character has been playing (The Arasaka Executive, The Watson Resident), the same device The Saurian Park uses for its "films of two centuries earlier". Otherwise the Civilizational Note names the mapping (The Voodoo Boys District, The Aldecaldos Convoy).
- **Sweep rulings.** No doctrine-sweep ruling names these four cards. DS-40 and DS-41 (The Ripperdoc Split) and the vessel-link ruling (The Fixer's Ledger) belong to other groups. No story here shows a vessel link, an upward move, a score band or an ascension application.
- **Charter check.** Every VMSS-side statement was read against the current Charter: Art. VI (the −3 federal floor), Art. III.III and III.VIII (taxation, the −3 treasury and its minimal federal infrastructure), Art. XXIV (the −3 floor: UBI distribution, taxation, federal law enforcement), Art. XXV.I–XXV.IV (clean energy, nuclear weapons, implant hacking, the two enforcement tracks), and the −1 and −3 dossiers. Two non-numeric conflicts were fixed (§1 and §3 flags). No story is stamped Off-canon and none is held.
- **Invented names** were checked against every `.html` page, `documents/` and the 24.4.x/24.5.x group files: Ingrid Rourke, Abel Lasko, Moberg, Luis Carrow, Pilar, Tomi, Celestine Marchaud, Jory Varek, Imre, Hollins and Esme Dunmore are all unused. Tobias Renn is canon (The Industrial Polluter, as it now reads on the site).

## 1. The Arasaka Executive

**Whose story:** Ingrid Rourke. Her tower's sovereignty is the judgments she signs, the Night City comparison reaches her over dinner, and the federal floor lands on her in a power-plant decision.

**Rewrite**

```text
The Arasaka Executive
Cyberpunk 2077 Element: Megacorp Towers · VMSS Analog: -1 Corporate Operator · Doctrine Snapshot: v16.5
Outcome: Hybrid Economy at Its Corporate Ceiling — Permitted, Watched, Bounded by Federal Law
```

Ingrid Rourke woke in the executive housing the corporation provided, and before she was out of bed her implant had laid the division's overnight figures across the ceiling. It was the corporate-issue model, with the corporate ledger overlay.

She ran a division of a megacorporation from a tower in -1. Inside the tower's perimeter the division was sovereign, as long as nothing inside it exported harm into the layers above. It paid -1's reduced taxes, and no Main Layer regulator looked at its internal commerce. That morning she signed two judgments from its corporate court: a machinist had broken the employment contract she had drafted, and a supplier had shorted a delivery. Her private security served both before lunch.

Her private capital was enormous and still growing. Two rungs of the corporate hierarchy sat above her, and the people on them were in no hurry to move.

On Thursdays she ate with the operators from the adjacent towers. After the second bottle Abel Lasko, who ran logistics two towers over, described an old game his junior had been playing, set in a place called Night City. The game's executives lived nearly as she did: towers, hierarchies, private security, a hybrid economy. The Arasaka tower there had no Main Layer enforcement above it, and it routinely commissioned weapons of civilizational-scale destructive capability. It ran covert cyberware programs that left its test subjects cyberpsychotic, and its intelligence operations went around whatever institutional oversight nominally existed.

"They had more fun," Abel said, and refilled her glass.

"We can't do any of that," Ingrid said.

On Friday a proposal for a new fabrication line reached her desk, with a power plant to run it. The engineers had costed two plants, and the cheaper one burned fuel and captured nothing. The federal floor bound her division at the same threshold as every other operator in -1: clean energy, nuclear weapons, implant hacking, organized sovereignty threats. Her counsel, Moberg, had attached the Industrial Polluter file: Tobias Renn, -1, a coal-fired plant.

"You remember this one," Moberg said. "Main Layer enforcement came in, shut the plant and seized his assets, and he went to -2." He turned the page toward her. "He had good lawyers too."

She struck the cheaper plant and signed for the other. On Monday the two people above her would see the difference in cost.

Civilizational Note: In -1, institutional governance steps back and private corporate governance fills the space. The federal floor still binds every operator regardless of capital, and Main Layer enforces it.

**Flags**

## 2. The Watson Resident

**Whose story:** Luis Carrow. The Main Layer provisions are his workday, his children's schooling and his son's birth, and Night City reaches him over his son's shoulder.

**Rewrite**

```text
The Watson Resident
Cyberpunk 2077 Element: Residential Middle Districts · VMSS Analog: Main Layer Under -1 Gravity · Doctrine Snapshot: v16.5
Outcome: Stable Daily Life on the Main Layer's Industrial Edge — Boring, Functional, Real
```

Luis Carrow's workday started at seven and ended at four. He was forty-one, a maintenance technician for a building services cooperative, and his mid-tier implant had the day's work orders sorted before he reached the first building. The work was steady, skilled and physical, and automation had not displaced it: the buildings he serviced were old enough that retrofitting them for full automation would cost more than continuing to pay him. The clean energy mandates had applied to every one of them for as long as he had been working.

He had lived seventeen years in the same Main Layer district, on the edge that abutted -1. From the apartment window he could see the towers of a major corporate center on the other side. He had never been inside one. No corporation over there had a contract with him, and he had never expected one to recruit him, threaten him or displace him. The district had picked up some of the towers' habits anyway: the late hours, the tower slang the kids used.

His wife, Pilar, taught pre-literacy at the community education center. Their two children went to school on the standard Main Layer educational provisions, with no conditions attached. His pay was the Main Layer median; the household wasn't wealthy and wasn't struggling. The cooperative's labour protections had covered him since his first day.

The evening news ran one of its occasional items about an enforcement action in -1 against a corporate operator who had crossed the federal floor.

"Is that one of ours?" Pilar asked, nodding at the window.

"Two over," Luis said, and went back to his soup.

After dinner their son Tomi played an old game set in a place called Night City, and Luis watched over his shoulder: towers, gangs on every block, and Watson, the residential half of the city. The people in the background there were neither corpos nor gangsters. They worked jobs, paid rent, rode the metro, bought groceries, watched the news and lived alongside the megacorps without being them. Most of the city was people like that, and the game rarely followed any of them home.

"What about them?" Luis asked.

"Nothing happens to them," Tomi said.

Tomi's character limped out of a clinic owing money. In Night City a bill like that followed a household for a decade. Luis thought of the night Pilar's pregnancy with Tomi went wrong, and of the Main Layer healthcare that had handled the complications.

"Did you have to pay for me?" Tomi asked.

"No," Luis said.

At seven the next morning he was in the basement of a building older than his grandfather, bleeding a radiator.

Civilizational Note: Main is the population layer. Separating it from the corporate towers of -1 and the wild districts of -3 keeps their consequences away from its households.

## 3. The Voodoo Boys District

**Whose story:** Celestine Marchaud, who hears the operators for the cooperative. Every permit and every refusal passes across her table, and the one enterprise the federal floor forbids comes from the cooperative's own netrunners.

**Rewrite**

```text
The Voodoo Boys District
Cyberpunk 2077 Element: Pacifica / Gang-Held Territory · VMSS Analog: -3 Punitive-Territorial Cooperative · Doctrine Snapshot: v16.5
Outcome: Self-Governing Territorial Enforcer — Wild Enterprise Tolerated, Civilizational Threats Refused
```

Celestine Marchaud heard operators out on the cooperative's behalf, in a room above the market. The cooperative had held its district of -3 for a long time, and the districts around it knew its name. Its territory held about forty thousand people and a contested set of utility connections to the federal floor's UBI distribution infrastructure. The private market below her ran on the cooperative's own currency-and-favor exchange. At the back of the building its netrunner cell sold data services to operators in the upper-layer commerce stream that reached -3 through cross-layer fixers. The cooperative did not call itself a government.

Jory Varek came to her the standard way, through an enforcer's cousin. He wanted to run a duel pit behind the old tannery.

"What does the district get?" Celestine asked.

He named the tribute. It was fair.

"Who have you run a pit for?"

"Hollins, out east." She had already asked around, and his name was good there.

"The tannery's two streets from the night market. Your crowd will empty those stalls."

"My crowd eats," Jory said. "They'll fill them."

Nothing about a duel pit would draw attention the cooperative didn't want.

"Do I get a paper?" he asked.

"No," Celestine said. "The enforcers won't come by."

Most operators who came the right way got that answer, whether they wanted a colosseum, a re-enactment environment, a drug market or a stunt operation. Plenty of stalls in the night market had never asked at all, and the cooperative had never bothered to stop them. The colosseum on the east road had asked, and the cooperative had put its own name over the gate. Some operators got a no, and the enforcers, the only enforcement in the district that mattered, shut them down.

In the spring the cell brought her the other kind. A buyer in the upper layers wanted implant hacking work, through a fixer, and the cell could do it.

"It pays better than anything we've sold," said Imre, who ran the cell.

"It leaves the district," Celestine said. "Then the federal response comes down on all forty thousand of us, and it's built to be overwhelming."

"Since when is the cooperative scared of anyone?"

"Only of them."

Imre read the terms again. "The fixer will want an answer."

Celestine pushed them back across the table. "Sell them something that stays down here."

She would have said the same to a nuclear weapons project, or to an organized threat to the broader civilization's sovereignty. Two enforcers sat in the cell's room that week until the fixer stopped calling.

That Friday Celestine went down to the tannery to watch a fight in Jory's pit.

Civilizational Note: Pacifica's Voodoo Boys are the kind of local, purchasable governance that -3 produces wherever a sufficiently organized population concentrates. VMSS makes it the only kind of structure available in -3 and holds it to the federal floor.
