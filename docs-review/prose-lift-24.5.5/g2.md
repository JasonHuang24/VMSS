**Conventions (same as the pilot and the 24.4.x / 24.5.x groups)**
- **Metadata blocks.** Title, Snapshot and the two middle fields are unchanged. - **Lesson box.** All three cards label it Civilizational Note. The label is kept and the box is held to the Key Lesson rules as far as the crossover allows: each note also carries the Night City side of the mapping, which puts them at 50–57 words.
- **Night City placement.** The bodies stay in-world, and every Night City name and fact sits in the Civilizational Note. This could go either way: a story could instead carry Night City in-body through a character who knows the old game. Whichever the section uses, it should use it on all seven cards.
- **Word counts.** A count covers the body plus the Civilizational Note (label included), excluding metadata. A word is any whitespace-separated token that contains a letter, digit or `$`. The originals were counted from `simulations.html` the same way.

## 1. The Cyberpsycho Trajectory

**Whose story:** Yusra Halvik, an -1 salvage-yard operator. Both flags and the lifted restriction land on her, and the -2 and -3 stages reach her through Kellan Varo's entries on the public ledger.

**Rewrite**

```text
The Cyberpsycho Trajectory
Cyberpunk 2077 Element: Cyberpsychosis · VMSS Analog: TIP Early Intervention or Terminal Reassignment · Doctrine Snapshot: v16.5
Outcome: Caught at -1, Reassigned at -2, Terminal at -3
```

Yusra Halvik ran a salvage yard in an -1 district, and for most of her working life she had done the lifting herself. When her back began to go, she started replacing what she lifted with: the left forearm in spring, the right in summer. In autumn she booked the shoulders.

That was when the implant ledger notified her care provider, who asked her to come in. Dr. Sefton had the usual speech ready and kept it short, but first she turned the screen so Yusra could read the notice.

*Augmentation acquisition: statistical threshold crossed. Associated with later behavioral decompensation. Informational.*

"Nobody's charging you with anything," Sefton said. "I'd like you to pause the installs for a while, and I want you to talk to someone." The referral was already written, to a counsellor who specialized in augmentation-pattern psychology.

Dr. Maelis saw her on a Thursday. In the waiting room a courier named Kellan Varo sat across from Yusra, turning a new hand over in his lap. At the end of the hour Maelis walked her to the door.

"Most people I only see once," she said.

"The shoulders are already paid for," Yusra said.

She had them done that winter at a clinic in the next district. The second notice came before the swelling went down.

*TIP-adjacent flag (prophylactic). Harm events: none. Trajectory endpoint: harm. Evidentiary trail: clear. Further cyberware certifications restricted.*

"Your existing configuration stays as it is," Maelis said the next Thursday. "It's only new work, and the restriction lifts once your pattern stabilises." She offered the more intensive counseling pathway, and Yusra took it.

Kellan was in the Thursday group with the same notice. "Restricted," he said the first week, flexing the new hand. "Like they'd know." By March he laughed in the wrong places when other people talked, and he couldn't sit through the hour.

Over the spring most of the group stopped. Somewhere in April Yusra lost the wish for the next install, and she only noticed it later. Kellan stopped coming.

In June she looked him up on the public ledger.

*Reassignment: -2. Event: assault; threat; destruction of property. Decompensation episode. Augmentation pattern entered in record.*

It read like every other -2 entry on the log. At the next group she asked Maelis what he would have down there.

"Revival," Maelis said. "Satellite-serviced. Victim restoration, if anyone hurts him." She said nothing else about him.

Her own restriction lifted in August: *pattern stabilised.* She pinned the notice above the desk in the yard office.

The next entry under his name came the following spring.

*Reassignment: -3. Further decompensation. Capital-level harm. Standard provisions.*

Yusra closed the screen and went out to the yard. A pallet of copper pipe was waiting by the gate, and she lifted it onto the truck.

In VMSS the ledger reads the same trajectory in Main and -1 long before anyone is hurt, and the cyberpsycho turns up only in -3, after every earlier stage has been refused.

## 2. The Ripperdoc Split

**Whose story:** Ferris Rook, the -1 ripperdoc. The flagged client lands in his chair, and the -3 side of the trade reaches him through Aldo Brek's call.


Ferris Rook kept his clinic in a converted pharmacy in an -1 district: two chairs, a sterilizer, and a bench by the window where people waited. No Main Layer regulator had any say over cyberware installation there, and he had never held a certificate. His overhead was low. His clients paid him directly, at rates his name could carry, and his name was the only assurance they got. His installations rarely failed. When a shoulder of his locked on a dockworker last spring, Ferris had it out and redone by evening and never sent a bill. His book was full into next season.

He had trained alongside Aldo Brek, on the same rigs, from the same manuals. Aldo had the better hands.

On a wet Tuesday Sal Okoro came in wanting forearm plating. Ferris had done Sal's eyes, and his brother's arms after that, and he asked after their mother while the intake ran. On the intake screen the ledger's line came up amber: *trajectory flagged, decompensation-adjacent.*

"Not this one," Ferris said.

"It's the same plating you put in my brother."

"Your brother isn't flagged."

Sal looked at the screen, then at him. "Somebody will do it."

"Somebody will."

Sal left without booking. Ferris wiped down the chair.

Years ago Aldo had filed for voluntary permanent residency and gone down to -3. He ran a back-alley clinic now, out of a fortified room in a cooperative-protected district, and whatever the cooperative tolerated, he could do. He had sent a picture of it once: steel shutters, the cooperative's mark painted on the door, and most of the same kit Ferris worked with. That night he called, as he did every few weeks.

"Paid the cooperative the usual tribute this morning," Aldo said. "Put combat ware in a punitive lad on Monday."

"Flagged?"

"Flagged. Paid up front." Aldo was eating something. "Nobody down here makes me act on the flags. There's a voluntary woman who wants a modification nobody's tried yet, and she's paying too."

"I turned one away today."

"Send him down."

Ferris didn't answer that.

"There's room for a second chair," Aldo said, "if you ever want it."

Ferris could have filed the same papers and followed him down if he had wanted to. He told Aldo it was raining.

In the morning the bench by the window was full.

VMSS sorts them by the jobs they refuse, whatever their skill or price, and Viktor Vektor would practise in -1.
